\chapter{Expressive State Transitions: DeltaProduct, Comba, RWKV-7}\label{ch:expressive}

The recurrent memories of \cref{ch:linear} and \cref{ch:delta} were judged mainly as
\emph{associative stores}: how many key--value pairs fit into the matrix state
$\mS_t\in\R^{d_v\times d_k}$, how cleanly an old association can be erased before a new one
is written, and how accurately a query retrieves what was stored. This chapter studies a
different memory capability, \emph{state tracking}. Think of a program that executes
\texttt{a, b = b, a} a thousand times, of a story in which characters keep swapping
objects, or of a chess game written as a list of moves. Nothing has to be retrieved
verbatim; instead the memory must hold a small state that is \emph{transformed} by every
token, and the final answer depends on the order of all the transformations. Whether a
recurrent model can do this is decided almost entirely by its \emph{state-transition
matrices}: the matrices that multiply the old state at every step.

We first build the necessary background from scratch
(\cref{sec:c06-background}): the general form of a matrix-state recurrence, a precise
definition of state tracking, a proof that diagonal transitions with positive entries cannot
even track parity, a short tour of the circuit-complexity argument (TC$^0$ versus NC$^1$)
that separates ``easy'' from ``hard'' state tracking, the eigenvalues of generalized
Householder matrices, and diagonal-plus-low-rank (DPLR) transitions. We then study three
2025 systems that enrich the transition while keeping training parallel:
DeltaProduct~\citep{siems2025deltaproduct} (\cref{sec:c06-deltaproduct}), which applies
several delta-rule steps per token so that every transition is a product of Householder
matrices; Comba~\citep{hu2025comba} (\cref{sec:c06-comba}), which uses a
scalar-plus-low-rank transition with state and output feedback; and RWKV-7
``Goose''~\citep{peng2025rwkv7} (\cref{sec:c06-rwkv7}), whose generalized delta rule has a
vector-valued decay, a vector-valued in-context learning rate and separate keys for removal
and replacement. \Cref{sec:c06-comparison} compares the three.

\section{Background: state tracking and the geometry of transitions}\label{sec:c06-background}

\subsection{Recap: one recurrence, many transition matrices}\label{sec:c06-recap}

All recurrent memories in this part of the book can be written, per head, as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:c06-general}
  \mS_t = \mS_{t-1}\mA_t + \mB_t, \qquad \vo_t = \mS_t\vq_t,
\end{equation}
with state $\mS_t\in\R^{d_v\times d_k}$, a \emph{transition matrix}
$\mA_t\in\R^{d_k\times d_k}$ that acts on the key side of the state, and a \emph{write}
$\mB_t\in\R^{d_v\times d_k}$. Both $\mA_t$ and $\mB_t$ are functions of the current token
only. \Cref{tab:c06-transitions} lists the choices made by the models of
\cref{ch:linear,ch:delta}. Unrolling \eqref{eq:c06-general} from $\mS_0$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:c06-unroll}
  \mS_t = \mS_0\,\mA_1\mA_2\cdots\mA_t + \sum_{s=1}^{t}\mB_s\,\mA_{s+1}\cdots\mA_t ,
\end{equation}
where an empty product is the identity. The write $\mB_s$ deposits information; the
products $\mA_{s+1}\cdots\mA_t$ decide what happens to it afterwards. In
\cref{ch:linear,ch:delta} we read these products as \emph{forgetting} (a decay
$\alpha_t<1$ shrinks old content) and \emph{erasing} (the delta rule removes the value
stored under the current key). In this chapter we read them as \emph{computation}: the
product of transition matrices is itself a summary of the input sequence, and what that
summary can represent is limited by the set of matrices that $\mA_t$ may take.

\paragraph{The delta rule as one step of online gradient descent.}
We will use one fact from \cref{ch:delta} repeatedly. Consider the regression loss
$\loss_t(\mS)=\tfrac12\norm{\mS\vk_t-\vv_t}^2$, which is small when the memory maps the key
$\vk_t$ to the value $\vv_t$. Its gradient with respect to $\mS$ is
$\nabla_{\mS}\loss_t=(\mS\vk_t-\vv_t)\vk_t\T$ (differentiate entry $(i,j)$:
$\partial\loss_t/\partial S_{ij}=(\mS\vk_t-\vv_t)_i\,k_{t,j}$). One gradient step with step
size $\beta_t$ is therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:c06-delta-gd}
  \mS_t=\mS_{t-1}-\beta_t(\mS_{t-1}\vk_t-\vv_t)\vk_t\T
       =\mS_{t-1}(\mI-\beta_t\vk_t\vk_t\T)+\beta_t\vv_t\vk_t\T ,
\end{equation}
which is the delta rule. The transition $\mI-\beta_t\vk_t\vk_t\T$ is the price of
correcting the memory, and it is \emph{not} diagonal: it mixes the key channels of the
state. This single fact is the source of the extra expressivity studied below.

\subsection{State tracking as a memory capability}\label{sec:c06-state-tracking}

\begin{definition}[State tracking]\label{def:c06-tracking}
Let $Q$ be a finite set of states, $\Sigma$ a finite input alphabet and
$\delta:Q\times\Sigma\to Q$ a transition function (a deterministic finite automaton). The
\emph{state-tracking} problem is: given $x_1,\dots,x_T\in\Sigma$ and a start state $s_0$,
output $s_t=\delta(s_{t-1},x_t)$ for every $t$. When the inputs are elements of a finite
group $G$ and $s_t=x_1x_2\cdots x_t$ is their running product, the problem is called the
\emph{word problem} for $G$.
\end{definition}

Three word problems recur in this chapter.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Parity}, the group $\mathbb{Z}_2=\{0,1\}$ under addition modulo~2: is the
number of ones seen so far odd?
\item \textbf{Counting modulo $m$}, the cyclic group $\mathbb{Z}_m$.
\item \textbf{Permutation composition}, the symmetric group $S_n$ of all permutations of
$n$ objects. Each token says ``swap the cups in positions 1 and 3'' or ``rotate the three
cups''; the state is the current arrangement. $S_3$ is the smallest group in which the
order of operations matters (it is not commutative); $S_5$ is the smallest symmetric group
that is not \emph{solvable}, which will matter in \cref{sec:c06-complexity}.
\end{itemize}
State tracking is a memory problem because the correct output depends on \emph{every}
earlier token and on their order, yet there is nothing to look up: the model must keep a
running summary and update it by composition. A Transformer can solve short instances by
attending to all earlier tokens, but its fixed depth limits how long a chain of
compositions it can evaluate (\cref{sec:c06-complexity}).

\paragraph{How a linear recurrence can track a group.}
Suppose each group element $g$ is assigned a $d\times d$ matrix $\rho(g)$ with
$\rho(g)\rho(h)=\rho(gh)$ (a \emph{matrix representation}). If the transition is
$\mA_t=\rho(x_t)$, the write is $\mB_t=\bm 0$ and $\mS_0=\mI$, then by
\eqref{eq:c06-unroll}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:c06-rep}
  \mS_t=\rho(x_1)\rho(x_2)\cdots\rho(x_t)=\rho(x_1x_2\cdots x_t),
\end{equation}
so the state \emph{is} the running product, and a query that compares $\mS_t$ with the
finitely many matrices $\rho(g)$ reads it out. Small examples:
\begin{itemize}
\item Parity: $\rho(0)=1$, $\rho(1)=-1$ (with $d=1$). The state is $(-1)^{\#\text{ones}}$.
\item $\mathbb{Z}_3$: $\rho(1)$ is the rotation of the plane by $120^\circ$.
\item $S_n$: $\rho(\sigma)$ is the $n\times n$ permutation matrix of $\sigma$.
\end{itemize}

\begin{keyidea}
A recurrent memory can track a group exactly when its transition matrices can realize a
representation of that group. Parity needs the eigenvalue $-1$; counting modulo $m$ needs
rotations (complex eigenvalues $e^{\pm 2\pi i/m}$); non-commutative groups such as $S_3$
and $S_5$ need transitions that do not commute with one another, hence cannot all be
diagonal in the same basis.
\end{keyidea}

\subsection{Why diagonal, positive transitions cannot track parity}\label{sec:c06-parity}

Most efficient recurrences of \cref{ch:linear} use diagonal transitions with entries in
$[0,1]$ (decays). The following proposition shows that such a layer cannot compute parity
for all lengths, however many channels it has. We prove it for one layer with a threshold
read-out; \citet{c03-grazzi2025unlocking} prove the general finite-precision statement for
multi-layer linear RNNs whose transitions have only non-negative eigenvalues.

\begin{proposition}[Positive diagonal transitions and parity]\label{prop:c06-parity}
Consider $\vh_t=\va(x_t)\had\vh_{t-1}+\vb(x_t)$ with $\vh_t\in\R^m$, inputs
$x_t\in\{0,1\}$, any functions $\va:\{0,1\}\to[0,1]^m$, $\vb:\{0,1\}\to\R^m$, and the
read-out $y_t=\mathbb{1}[\bm{c}\T\vh_t>\theta]$. Then on the all-ones input $1^n$ the output
$y_n$ is eventually constant in $n$ (up to a measure-zero tie $\bm{c}\T\vh^\star=\theta$). In
particular no such model outputs the parity of $1^n$ for every $n$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
On the input $1^n$ the coefficients are constant: $\va=\va(1)$, $\vb=\vb(1)$. Treat each
channel $i$ separately.
If $a_i<1$, let $h_i^\star=b_i/(1-a_i)$ be the fixed point. Subtracting
$h_i^\star=a_ih_i^\star+b_i$ from $h_{n,i}=a_ih_{n-1,i}+b_i$ gives
$h_{n,i}-h_i^\star=a_i(h_{n-1,i}-h_i^\star)$, hence
$h_{n,i}=h_i^\star+a_i^{\,n}(h_{0,i}-h_i^\star)\to h_i^\star$.
If $a_i=1$, then $h_{n,i}=h_{0,i}+nb_i$, a linear function of $n$.
So $f(n)=\bm{c}\T\vh_n-\theta=\gamma_0+\gamma_1 n+\varepsilon(n)$ with $\varepsilon(n)\to0$,
where $\gamma_1=\sum_{i:a_i=1}c_ib_i$. If $\gamma_1\neq0$ the sign of $f(n)$ is
eventually the sign of $\gamma_1$. If $\gamma_1=0$ then $f(n)\to\gamma_0$, and if
$\gamma_0\ne0$ the sign is eventually that of $\gamma_0$. In both cases $y_n$ stops
changing, while parity alternates forever.
\end{proof}

The argument has a simple reading: with non-negative decays every channel moves
\emph{monotonically} toward a fixed point (or away from it at constant speed); nothing in the
state can oscillate. A deeper network does not help: if the input to a layer is eventually
constant, the same argument applies to that layer, and in finite precision a convergent
state becomes \emph{exactly} constant after finitely many steps, so the next layer also sees
an eventually constant input. This induction is the core of the finite-precision theorem of
\citet{c03-grazzi2025unlocking}. Our numerical check (2000 random four-channel models on
$1^{400}$) found at most two sign changes of the read-out, whereas parity needs 399.

A single negative eigenvalue repairs this. Take $m=1$, $a(0)=1$, $a(1)=-1$, $b\equiv0$
and $h_0=1$: then $h_n=(-1)^{\#\text{ones}}$ and the sign of the state is the parity. In
delta-rule form this is \eqref{eq:c06-delta-gd} with $d_k=d_v=1$, $k_t=1$, $v_t=0$ and
$\beta_t=2x_t$: the transition $1-\beta_t$ is $+1$ for a zero and $-1$ for a one. Our check
tracked parity without error over $10^5$ random bits this way.

\subsection{A circuit-complexity view: TC\texorpdfstring{$^0$}{0} versus NC\texorpdfstring{$^1$}{1}}\label{sec:c06-complexity}

Parity is not a ``hard'' problem; it failed above only because of the sign of the
eigenvalues. A second, deeper limitation concerns \emph{non-commutative} composition. It is
best explained with Boolean circuits, which model parallel computation: the \emph{size} of
a circuit is its number of gates and its \emph{depth} is the length of the longest path
from an input to the output, i.e.\ the parallel running time.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{NC$^1$}: problems solvable by circuits of polynomial size and depth
$\bigO(\log n)$ built from AND, OR and NOT gates with two inputs each.
\item \textbf{TC$^0$}: problems solvable by circuits of polynomial size and \emph{constant}
depth, where gates may have any number of inputs and may be \emph{threshold} (majority)
gates, which output 1 when enough of their inputs are 1.
\end{itemize}
Threshold gates can count, so TC$^0$ contains addition and multiplication of integers,
counting, and parity. It is known that TC$^0\subseteq$ NC$^1$, and it is widely believed,
though unproven, that the inclusion is strict. The canonical NC$^1$-hard problem is a word
problem: \citet{c06-barrington1989} showed that the word problem for $S_5$ (more generally,
for any non-solvable group) is complete for NC$^1$. Intuitively, composing permutations of
five objects is ``inherently sequential'': unlike sums, which can be regrouped and counted,
the result depends on the order in a way no constant-depth threshold circuit can capture,
unless TC$^0=$ NC$^1$.

Where do sequence models sit?
\begin{itemize}
\item Transformers with log-precision arithmetic can be simulated by uniform TC$^0$
circuits~\citep{c06-merrill2023parallelism}. So, under the standard conjecture, a
fixed-depth Transformer cannot solve the $S_5$ word problem for all lengths.
\item \citet{c06-merrill2024illusion} show the same for state-space models such as S4 and
Mamba, whose transitions are diagonal (or fixed): their ``state'' is in this sense an
illusion. The intuition: with diagonal transitions, every entry of the state is a sum of
terms $b_s\prod_{r=s+1}^{t}a_r$; products of scalars are sums of logarithms, and iterated
sums and products of numbers are computable in TC$^0$.
\item Non-diagonal, input-dependent transitions escape this argument. Multiplying
$t$ permutation matrices \emph{is} the $S_5$ word problem. Such a product can still be
computed in parallel by a tree of pairwise multiplications (matrix multiplication is
associative), in depth $\bigO(\log T)$, which places these models naturally in NC$^1$.
This is exactly the regime of DeltaNet with negative eigenvalues, DeltaProduct and RWKV-7.
\end{itemize}
Two cautions keep this picture honest. First, these are statements about exact solutions
for \emph{all} sequence lengths; for bounded lengths a deep enough Transformer or SSM can
learn shortcuts. Second, the bounds concern what an architecture \emph{can} represent, not
what training finds. Still, the theory makes a sharp, testable prediction, and the
experiments in the DeltaProduct repository follow it: with eigenvalues restricted to $[0,1]$,
or with too few Householder factors per token, a single layer fails on $S_3$ and $S_5$, while
enough factors with eigenvalues in $[-1,1]$ succeed (\cref{sec:c06-dp-results}).

\subsection{Eigenvalues of generalized Householder matrices}\label{sec:c06-householder}

\begin{definition}[Generalized Householder matrix]\label{def:c06-householder}
For a unit vector $\vk\in\R^{d}$ ($\norm{\vk}=1$) and a scalar $\beta$, let
$\mH(\vk,\beta)=\mI-\beta\vk\vk\T$. For $\beta=2$ this is the Householder
reflection~\citep{c06-householder1958} across the hyperplane orthogonal to $\vk$.
\end{definition}

The spectrum follows from two one-line computations. For the key itself,
$\mH\vk=\vk-\beta\vk(\vk\T\vk)=(1-\beta)\vk$. For any $\vu\perp\vk$,
$\mH\vu=\vu-\beta\vk(\vk\T\vu)=\vu$. Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:c06-hh-eig}
  \text{eigenvalues of }\mH(\vk,\beta):\quad \underbrace{1,\dots,1}_{d-1\text{ times}},\;1-\beta .
\end{equation}
Since $\mH$ is symmetric, its spectral norm is the largest absolute eigenvalue,
$\norm{\mH}_2=\max(1,\abs{1-\beta})$, which is at most one exactly when
$\beta\in[0,2]$. Three regimes matter (our check confirmed the eigenvalues numerically):
\begin{itemize}
\item $\beta\in[0,1]$ (DeltaNet and Gated DeltaNet with $\beta_t=\sigmoid(\cdot)$):
eigenvalue $1-\beta\in[0,1]$. At $\beta=1$ the memory under $\vk$ is erased and replaced
(a projection).
\item $\beta\in(1,2)$: eigenvalue in $(-1,0)$. The memory \emph{overshoots}.
\item $\beta=2$: eigenvalue $-1$, an orthogonal reflection.
\end{itemize}
The memory interpretation of a negative eigenvalue is instructive. Multiply
\eqref{eq:c06-delta-gd} by $\vk_t$ and subtract $\vv_t$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:c06-residual}
  \mS_t\vk_t-\vv_t=(1-\beta_t)\,(\mS_{t-1}\vk_t-\vv_t).
\end{equation}
The retrieval error for the current key shrinks by the factor $1-\beta_t$. For
$\beta_t\in(0,1)$ the correction stops short of the target, for $\beta_t=1$ it hits it, and
for $\beta_t\in(1,2)$ it passes the target and flips the sign of the error, while still
reducing its size. This is the familiar stability condition of gradient descent on a
quadratic with curvature $\norm{\vk_t}^2=1$: step sizes up to $2/\text{curvature}$ converge.
Allowing $\beta_t\in(0,2)$ is therefore a natural extension of the online-learning view,
and in code it is literally one line: \texttt{beta = beta * 2}~\citep{c03-grazzi2025unlocking}.

\begin{pitfall}
The eigenvalue along $\vk$ is $1-\beta\norm{\vk}^2$, not $1-\beta$. All implementations in
this chapter normalize keys (\code{use_qk_l2norm_in_kernel=True} in \fla); without unit
keys, doubling $\beta$ can push the eigenvalue below $-1$ and make the recurrence explode.
\end{pitfall}

\paragraph{Products of Householder matrices.}
A single generalized Householder matrix is symmetric, so all its eigenvalues are real. Their
products are not symmetric and can have complex eigenvalues. The classical example
(proved in \cref{ex:c06-rotation}) is that two reflections compose to a rotation: if $\vk_1,\vk_2$
are unit vectors at angle $\theta$, then $\mH(\vk_1,2)\mH(\vk_2,2)$ rotates the plane
$\mathrm{span}\{\vk_1,\vk_2\}$ by $2\theta$ and leaves its orthogonal complement fixed, with
eigenvalues $e^{\pm 2i\theta}$ there (our check confirmed this to $10^{-16}$). Two
reflections whose normals are $60^\circ$ apart give a rotation $\bm{R}$ by $120^\circ$ with
$\bm{R}^3=\mI$, a representation of $\mathbb{Z}_3$. More generally, the
Cartan--Dieudonn\'e theorem states that every orthogonal $d\times d$ matrix is a product of
at most $d$ reflections, so products of Householders can reach every rotation and every
permutation.

Two facts make long products cheap. First, a product of $n$ generalized Householders is
the identity minus a matrix of rank at most $n$. Writing
$\prod_{j=1}^{n}(\mI-\beta_j\vk_j\vk_j\T)=\mI-\sum_{j=1}^{n}\vw_j\vk_j\T$ and multiplying
one more factor on the right shows, by induction, that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:c06-wy}
  \vw_j=\beta_j\Big(\vk_j-\sum_{i<j}\vw_i\,(\vk_i\T\vk_j)\Big),
\end{equation}
the WY representation~\citep{c03-bischof1987wy} that \cref{ch:linear} turns into the
chunkwise ``UT transform''. Second, applying the product to a state never requires forming
it: $n$ rank-one updates cost $\bigO(n\,d_kd_v)$. Finally, every factor with
$\beta_j\in[0,2]$ has norm at most one, so the product has norm at most one and all its
eigenvalues lie in the closed unit disk: products of stable Householders are stable.

\paragraph{Exact permutation tracking.}
The next proposition, which we derive here because it explains the experiments of the
DeltaProduct repository, pins down exactly how many Householder factors a transition needs
in order to \emph{be} a permutation matrix.

\begin{proposition}[Permutations as Householder products]\label{prop:c06-perm}
Let $\mP$ be the $n\times n$ permutation matrix of $\sigma\in S_n$ and let $c(\sigma)$ be the
number of cycles of $\sigma$, counting fixed points. Suppose
$\mP=\prod_{j=1}^{m}\mH(\vk_j,\beta_j)$ with unit $\vk_j$ and $\beta_j\in[0,2]$. Then
(a) every $\beta_j\in\{0,2\}$, i.e.\ every non-trivial factor is an exact reflection;
(b) $m\ge n-c(\sigma)$; and (c) $m=n-c(\sigma)$ factors suffice. Hence $n-1$ factors
represent every element of $S_n$, and $n-1$ are needed for an $n$-cycle.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(a) Determinants multiply, and by \eqref{eq:c06-hh-eig}
$\det\mH(\vk,\beta)=1-\beta$. A permutation matrix has $\det\mP=\pm1$, so
$\prod_j\abs{1-\beta_j}=1$. Each factor satisfies $\abs{1-\beta_j}\le1$ when
$\beta_j\in[0,2]$, so every factor must equal one in absolute value: $\beta_j\in\{0,2\}$.
(b) By \eqref{eq:c06-wy}, $\mI-\mP=\sum_{j}\vw_j\vk_j\T$ has rank at most $m$. The kernel of
$\mI-\mP$ is the set of vectors fixed by the permutation, i.e.\ vectors that are constant
on each cycle, a space of dimension $c(\sigma)$; so $\rank(\mI-\mP)=n-c(\sigma)\le m$.
(c) A transposition $(i\,j)$ is the reflection with $\vk=(\ve_i-\ve_j)/\sqrt2$ and $\beta=2$:
indeed $(\mI-(\ve_i-\ve_j)(\ve_i-\ve_j)\T)\ve_i=\ve_i-(\ve_i-\ve_j)=\ve_j$, symmetrically
$\ve_j\mapsto\ve_i$, and every other basis vector is orthogonal to $\ve_i-\ve_j$ and fixed. A
cycle of length $\ell$ is a product of $\ell-1$ transpositions, so $\sigma$ is a product of
$\sum(\ell-1)=n-c(\sigma)$ transpositions.
\end{proof}

Part (a) explains why eigenvalues in $[0,1]$ are useless for exact permutation tracking no
matter how many factors are used (every factor would need $\beta_j=0$). Part (b)--(c)
predicts that one layer tracks $S_3$ with two factors per token and $S_5$ with four. We
verified the construction numerically: with transposition reflections and $v=0$, a state
initialized at $\mI$ tracked random words of length 2000 over $S_3$, $S_4$ and $S_5$ with no
error, using at most 2, 3 and 4 delta steps per token. A single factor is not enough for a
3-cycle for a simpler reason as well: a 3-cycle has the complex eigenvalues
$e^{\pm2\pi i/3}$, while $\mH(\vk,\beta)$ is symmetric with real eigenvalues.

\subsection{Diagonal-plus-low-rank transitions}\label{sec:c06-dplr}

The transitions of \cref{tab:c06-transitions} share one structure: a diagonal matrix plus
a matrix of low rank,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:c06-dplr}
  \mA_t=\mD_t+\bm\phi_t\bm\psi_t\T,\qquad \mD_t \text{ diagonal},\ \bm\phi_t,\bm\psi_t\in\R^{d_k}
\end{equation}
(DPLR; with $n$ rank-one terms for DeltaProduct). The \fla{} library names the special
cases \emph{IPLR} ($\mD_t=\mI$, identity plus low rank) and DPLR; Comba calls the case
$\mD_t=\alpha_t\mI$ \emph{SPLR} (scalar plus low rank). DeltaNet is IPLR with
$\bm\phi_t=-\beta_t\vk_t$, $\bm\psi_t=\vk_t$; Gated DeltaNet is SPLR with
$\bm\phi_t=-\alpha_t\beta_t\vk_t$, $\bm\psi_t=\vk_t$; KDA is DPLR with
$\mD_t=\diag(\valpha_t)$, $\bm\phi_t=-\beta_t\valpha_t\had\vk_t$, $\bm\psi_t=\vk_t$; RWKV-7 is DPLR with
$\bm\phi_t=-\hat{\bm\kappa}_t$, $\bm\psi_t=\va_t\had\hat{\bm\kappa}_t$.

DPLR matrices are attractive because (i) multiplying a state by one costs $\bigO(d_kd_v)$,
like a diagonal transition, (ii) unlike a diagonal, they mix channels and need not commute
with each other, and (iii) products of them inside a chunk have a compact
WY-like form, which gives chunkwise-parallel training (we derive the DPLR version in
\cref{sec:c06-rwkv-formulation}). Their eigenvalues, however, are not automatically
controlled. For the identity-plus-rank-one case the answer is simple: $\mI+\bm\phi\bm\psi\T$
fixes every vector orthogonal to $\bm\psi$, and $(\mI+\bm\phi\bm\psi\T)\bm\phi=
(1+\bm\psi\T\bm\phi)\bm\phi$, so the eigenvalues are $1$ (with multiplicity $d-1$) and
$1+\bm\psi\T\bm\phi$. To stay in $[-1,1]$ one needs $\bm\psi\T\bm\phi\in[-2,0]$, which is why
the \fla{} documentation (\repofile{repos/fla-org__flash-linear-attention/fla/ops/generalized_delta_rule/README.md})
warns that $\bm\phi$ and $\bm\psi$ must point in opposite directions. With a non-identity
diagonal no such closed form exists in general; each system in this chapter restricts the
structure so that the spectrum stays controlled, and we analyze each one in turn.

\section{DeltaProduct: several delta steps per token}\label{sec:c06-deltaproduct}

\subsection{Motivation}

DeltaNet with $\beta_t\in(0,2)$ can realize one reflection per token, which is enough for
parity (\cref{sec:c06-parity}) but, by \cref{prop:c06-perm}, not for a 3-cycle, let alone
for $S_5$. Its transition is identity plus \emph{rank one}, and a rank-one change of the
identity can only move one direction of the state. DeltaProduct~\citep{siems2025deltaproduct}
asks: since the delta rule is one step of online gradient descent on
$\tfrac12\norm{\mS\vk-\vv}^2$ (\cref{eq:c06-delta-gd}), why not take \emph{several} steps per
token, each with its own key, value and step size? The transition then becomes a product
of $n_h$ generalized Householder matrices, identity plus rank $n_h$, and $n_h$ becomes a
dial between the cost of DeltaNet ($n_h=1$) and the expressivity of arbitrary orthogonal
transitions ($n_h=d_k$). Crucially, the state size does not change: DeltaProduct buys
expressivity with computation, not with memory.

\begin{taxonomy}
Following \citet{zhoubian2026memory}: \textbf{representation} implicit (a fixed-size
recurrent state inside the forward pass); \textbf{update dynamics} online (written at every
token during inference); \textbf{persistence}: DeltaProduct is discussed in the survey's
\S III-C (``Expressive Delta and Bilinear Updates'') but not listed in its Table~I; by the
survey's criteria for the related Gated DeltaNet it would be long-term (our
classification); \textbf{update rule} (Table~II): state-transition update, here a product of
$n_h$ delta-rule corrections per token.
\end{taxonomy}

\subsection{Formulation}\label{sec:c06-dp-formulation}

\paragraph{Per-token recurrence.}
At token $t$ the layer produces one query $\vq_t$, a forget gate $\alpha_t\in(0,1]$ and
$n_h$ triples $(\vk_{t,j},\vv_{t,j},\beta_{t,j})$, $j=1,\dots,n_h$, with unit keys and
$\beta_{t,j}\in(0,2)$ (or $(0,1)$ if negative eigenvalues are disabled). Starting from the
decayed state $\mS_{t,0}=\alpha_t\mS_{t-1}$, it takes $n_h$ gradient steps, step $j$ on the
loss $\tfrac12\norm{\mS\vk_{t,j}-\vv_{t,j}}^2$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:c06-dp-step}
  \mS_{t,j}=\mS_{t,j-1}-\beta_{t,j}(\mS_{t,j-1}\vk_{t,j}-\vv_{t,j})\vk_{t,j}\T
          =\mS_{t,j-1}\mH_{t,j}+\beta_{t,j}\vv_{t,j}\vk_{t,j}\T,
\end{equation}
with $\mH_{t,j}=\mI-\beta_{t,j}\vk_{t,j}\vk_{t,j}\T$. The new state is
$\mS_t=\mS_{t,n_h}$ and the read-out is $\vo_t=\mS_t\vq_t$, \emph{after} all $n_h$ steps.

\paragraph{The equivalent one-step form.}
Unrolling the $n_h$ inner steps exactly as in \eqref{eq:c06-unroll} gives the standard form
$\mS_t=\mS_{t-1}\mA_t+\mB_t$ with
\begin{align}
  \mA_t&=\alpha_t\,\mH_{t,1}\mH_{t,2}\cdots\mH_{t,n_h}, \label{eq:c06-dp-A}\\
  \mB_t&=\sum_{j=1}^{n_h}\beta_{t,j}\vv_{t,j}\vk_{t,j}\T\,\mH_{t,j+1}\cdots\mH_{t,n_h}. \label{eq:c06-dp-B}
\end{align}
By \eqref{eq:c06-wy}, $\mA_t=\alpha_t(\mI-\sum_j\vw_{t,j}\vk_{t,j}\T)$ is a scalar times
identity-plus-rank-$n_h$, which is the ``diagonal plus rank $n_h$'' structure the paper
emphasizes (it uses a scalar gate; with a diagonal gate it would be DPLR). Every factor has
norm at most one when $\beta_{t,j}\in[0,2]$, so $\norm{\mA_t}_2\le\alpha_t\le1$ and the
eigenvalues lie in the disk of radius $\alpha_t$; unlike DeltaNet they can be complex. Our
check confirmed \eqref{eq:c06-dp-A}--\eqref{eq:c06-dp-B} to $10^{-16}$, that
$\rank(\mI-\mH_{t,1}\cdots\mH_{t,n_h})=n_h$ for generic keys, and that the spectral norm of
the product is one.

\paragraph{Special cases.}
With $n_h=1$ the recurrence is exactly the gated delta rule (our check: zero difference).
With $\alpha_t\equiv1$ it is the ungated DeltaProduct. With $\beta_{t,j}\in(0,1)$ it is
DeltaProduct$_{n_h}[0,1]$ in the paper's notation, with $\beta_{t,j}\in(0,2)$ it is
DeltaProduct$_{n_h}[-1,1]$: the bracket names the eigenvalue range of each factor.

\paragraph{Expressivity.}
By \cref{prop:c06-perm}, DeltaProduct$_{n_h}[-1,1]$ can realize every permutation of
$n_h+1$ objects as a single transition, so one layer can track $S_{n_h+1}$ by
\eqref{eq:c06-rep}; with $[0,1]$ factors it can realize no non-trivial permutation at all.
By the Cartan--Dieudonn\'e theorem, $n_h=d_k$ reaches every orthogonal transition. The
paper goes further: it characterizes state tracking in finite precision and how it improves
with $n_h$ and with depth (see the paper for the exact theorems), and it reads the
improvement in language modeling partly as better length extrapolation.

\paragraph{How the $n_h$ keys, values and step sizes are produced.}
Each token embedding $\vx_t$ is projected separately for each step $j$, followed by the
usual short causal convolution and SiLU (the same pipeline as Gated DeltaNet in
\cref{ch:delta}):
\begin{equation}\label{eq:c06-dp-proj}
  \vk_{t,j}=\frac{\SiLU(\mathrm{conv}(\mW_{K,j}\T\vx_t))}{\norm{\cdot}},\quad
  \vv_{t,j}=\SiLU(\mathrm{conv}(\mW_{V,j}\T\vx_t)),\quad
  \beta_{t,j}=c\,\sigmoid(\vw_{\beta,j}\T\vx_t),
\end{equation}
with $c=2$ when negative eigenvalues are allowed and $c=1$ otherwise. The query and the gate
are produced once per token; the gate has the Mamba-2 form
$\log\alpha_t=-e^{A}\,\mathrm{softplus}(\vw_\alpha\T\vx_t+b_{\Delta})$ with learned scalars
$A$ and $b_\Delta$ per head. The extra parameters are $(n_h-1)$ copies of the key, value and
$\beta$ projections; the parameter-matched configurations in the official repository
compensate by using fewer heads (\cref{sec:c06-dp-code}).

\paragraph{Chunkwise form by interleaving.}
The inner loop \eqref{eq:c06-dp-step} is just the gated delta rule run on a sequence that is
$n_h$ times longer. Define the interleaved sequence of length $Tn_h$, indexed by
$\tau=(t-1)n_h+j$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:c06-interleave}
  \tilde\vk_\tau=\vk_{t,j},\quad \tilde\vv_\tau=\vv_{t,j},\quad
  \tilde\beta_\tau=\beta_{t,j},\quad
  \log\tilde\alpha_\tau=\begin{cases}\log\alpha_t & j=1\\ 0 & j>1\end{cases},\quad
  \tilde\vq_\tau=\begin{cases}\vq_t & j=n_h\\ \bm 0 & j<n_h\end{cases}.
\end{equation}
Run the gated delta rule
$\tilde\mS_\tau=\tilde\alpha_\tau\tilde\mS_{\tau-1}(\mI-\tilde\beta_\tau\tilde\vk_\tau\tilde\vk_\tau\T)
+\tilde\beta_\tau\tilde\vv_\tau\tilde\vk_\tau\T$ and keep the outputs
$\tilde\vo_{tn_h}$, $t=1,\dots,T$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:c06-interleave}
$\tilde\mS_{tn_h}=\mS_t$ and $\tilde\vo_{tn_h}=\vo_t$ for all $t$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
By induction on $t$; $t=0$ is trivial ($\tilde\mS_0=\mS_0$). Assume
$\tilde\mS_{(t-1)n_h}=\mS_{t-1}$. At $\tau=(t-1)n_h+1$ the gate is $\alpha_t$ and the update
is step $j=1$ of \eqref{eq:c06-dp-step} applied to $\alpha_t\mS_{t-1}=\mS_{t,0}$. At the
following $n_h-1$ positions the gate is $e^0=1$, so the updates are exactly steps
$j=2,\dots,n_h$. Hence $\tilde\mS_{tn_h}=\mS_{t,n_h}=\mS_t$, and the output at that position
uses $\tilde\vq_{tn_h}=\vq_t$. Outputs at the other positions use a zero query and are
discarded.
\end{proof}

Consequently every chunkwise algorithm for the gated delta rule (the WY/UT transform of
\cref{ch:linear,ch:delta}) trains DeltaProduct unchanged. For completeness we restate the
one we checked numerically, in our notation. Split the interleaved sequence into chunks of
length $C$. Inside a chunk with start state $\mS_{[i]}$, let
$\gamma_r=\prod_{s\le r}\tilde\alpha_s$ (cumulative within the chunk) and let
$\vu_r$ be the ``pseudo-value'' written at position $r$, so that
$\tilde\mS_r=\gamma_r\mS_{[i]}+\sum_{s\le r}(\gamma_r/\gamma_s)\vu_s\tilde\vk_s\T$. Matching
with the recurrence gives
$\vu_r=\tilde\beta_r(\tilde\vv_r-\gamma_r\mS_{[i]}\tilde\vk_r-\sum_{s<r}(\gamma_r/\gamma_s)(\tilde\vk_s\T\tilde\vk_r)\vu_s)$,
a lower-triangular linear system. In matrix form, with
$(\mT)_{rs}=\tilde\beta_r(\gamma_r/\gamma_s)\tilde\vk_r\T\tilde\vk_s$ for $s<r$ (zero
otherwise) and $\mM=(\mI+\mT)^{-1}$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:c06-ut}
  \mU=\mM\diag(\tilde\vbeta)\tilde\mV-\mM\diag(\tilde\vbeta\had\bm{\gamma})\tilde\mK\,\mS_{[i]}\T,
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:c06-ut-out}
  \tilde\mO_{[i]}=\diag(\bm{\gamma})\tilde\mQ\mS_{[i]}\T+\big((\tilde\mQ\tilde\mK\T)\had\bm{\Gamma}\big)_{\le}\mU,\qquad
  \mS_{[i+1]}=\gamma_C\mS_{[i]}+\mU\T\diag(\gamma_C/\bm{\gamma})\tilde\mK,
\end{equation}
where $\bm{\Gamma}_{rs}=\gamma_r/\gamma_s$ and $(\cdot)_{\le}$ keeps the lower triangle including
the diagonal. Our NumPy implementation of \eqref{eq:c06-ut}--\eqref{eq:c06-ut-out} on the
interleaved sequence matched the DeltaProduct recurrence to $3\times10^{-15}$ for chunk sizes
4, 8 and 16.

\subsection{Algorithms and complexity}

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{DeltaProduct (one head): decoding step and chunkwise prefill}
\label{alg:c06-deltaproduct}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require state $\mS\in\R^{d_v\times d_k}$; gate $\alpha_t$; query $\vq_t$; triples $(\vk_{t,j},\vv_{t,j},\beta_{t,j})_{j=1}^{n_h}$
\Statex \textbf{Decoding one token}
\State $\mS\gets\alpha_t\mS$ \Comment{decay once per token}
\For{$j=1,\dots,n_h$}
  \State $\ve\gets\mS\vk_{t,j}-\vv_{t,j}$ \Comment{prediction error, $\bigO(d_kd_v)$}
  \State $\mS\gets\mS-\beta_{t,j}\,\ve\,\vk_{t,j}\T$ \Comment{one online gradient step}
\EndFor
\State \Return $\vo_t=\mS\vq_t$ and $\mS$
\Statex \textbf{Prefill of a sequence of $T$ tokens}
\State Build $(\tilde\mQ,\tilde\mK,\tilde\mV,\tilde\vbeta,\log\tilde\valpha)$ of length $Tn_h$ by \eqref{eq:c06-interleave}
\For{each chunk $[i]$ of $C$ interleaved positions}
  \State Solve \eqref{eq:c06-ut} for the pseudo-values $\mU$ (one $C\times C$ triangular inverse)
  \State Compute the chunk outputs and $\mS_{[i+1]}$ by \eqref{eq:c06-ut-out}
\EndFor
\State \Return rows $n_h,2n_h,\dots,Tn_h$ of $\tilde\mO$ and the final state
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\Cref{alg:c06-deltaproduct} summarizes both modes. Per head:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{State size}: $d_kd_v$, the same as DeltaNet for every $n_h$.
\item \textbf{Decoding}: $n_h$ rank-one updates and one read, $\bigO(n_h\,d_kd_v)$ time per
token (about $n_h$ times Gated DeltaNet).
\item \textbf{Prefill}: the chunkwise gated delta rule on $Tn_h$ positions costs
$\bigO(Tn_h(Cd_k+Cd_v+d_kd_v))$ time plus $\bigO(Tn_hC^2)$ for the triangular inverses, i.e.\
roughly $n_h$ times Gated DeltaNet. The sequential depth is $Tn_h/C$ chunk steps.
\item \textbf{Parameters}: $n_h$ key, value and $\beta$ projections instead of one.
\end{itemize}
The trade-off is thus linear: each extra Householder costs about one Gated DeltaNet's worth of
compute and projection parameters, and adds one more direction that the transition can
reflect or rotate.

\subsection{Implementation walkthrough}\label{sec:c06-dp-code}

There are two code bases: the experiment code of the paper
(\repo{automl__DeltaProduct}), which contains a snapshot of \fla{} with a first
DeltaProduct layer, and the maintained version that the authors contributed to \fla{}
(\repo{fla-org__flash-linear-attention}). The repository README says that the \fla{} version
is the one that will be kept up to date.

\begin{implnote}[title={In the code: the official experiment layer}]
The layer \code{GatedDeltaProduct} in
\repofile{repos/automl__DeltaProduct/flash-linear-attention/fla/layers/gated_deltaproduct.py}
keeps $n_h$ separate \code{nn.Linear} modules in \code{k_projs}, \code{v_projs},
\code{b_projs} (one per Householder, controlled by \code{num_householder}) and $n_h$ short
convolutions. Its forward pass implements \cref{prop:c06-interleave} literally with the helper
\code{interleave_multiple_sequences}, which stacks $n_h$ tensors of shape
\texttt{[B, T, ...]} along a new axis and reshapes to \texttt{[B, T*n\_h, ...]}, and then calls the
ordinary \code{chunk_gated_delta_rule} kernel (or \code{chunk_delta_rule} without the forget
gate) with \code{use_qk_l2norm_in_kernel=True}.
\end{implnote}

\repofile{repos/fla-org__flash-linear-attention/fla/layers/gated_deltaproduct.py} fuses the
$n_h$ projections into single matrices: \code{k_proj} maps \code{hidden_size} to
\texttt{key\_dim * num\_householder}, \code{v_proj} to \texttt{value\_dim * num\_householder} and

then produces the interleaved key and value sequences of length $Tn_h$ directly. Layer
defaults: \code{num_householder=2}, \code{allow_neg_eigval=True},
\code{use_forget_gate=True}, \code{head_dim=256}, \code{expand_v=2}; the model configuration
\code{GatedDeltaProductConfig} defaults to \code{num_householder=1},
\code{allow_neg_eigval=False}, \code{use_forget_gate=False}, so a model must set them
explicitly. The gate is the Mamba-2 parameterization
\texttt{g = -A\_log.exp() * softplus(a\_proj(x) + dt\_bias)} with one value per head and token.
Training and prefill call \code{chunk_gated_delta_product}; short inputs at inference
($T\le64$, no gradients) call \code{fused_recurrent_gated_delta_rule} on the zero-padded
interleaved sequence and slice the last sub-step, as in the official layer.
\end{implnote}

\begin{implnote}[title={In the code: the dedicated \fla{} kernels}]
The directory \repofile{repos/fla-org__flash-linear-attention/fla/ops/gated_delta_product/}
holds a reference recurrence (\code{naive.py}), a reference that calls the gated delta rule
on the interleaved sequence (\code{chunk_ref.py}), and the Triton path (\code{chunk.py},
\code{chunk_deltaproduct_h.py}, \code{chunk_deltaproduct_o.py}). Tensors use the \fla{}
layout: queries \texttt{[B, T, H, K]}, keys and values \texttt{[B, T*n\_h, H, K]} and
\texttt{[B, T*n\_h, H, V]}, \code{beta} \texttt{[B, T*n\_h, H]}, the log-gate \code{g}
\texttt{[B, T, H]}, and the state \texttt{[B, H, K, V]}, which is the \emph{transpose} of our
$\mS_t$. The forward pass \code{chunk_gated_delta_product_fwd} goes through four stages:
\begin{enumerate}
\item The per-token gate is scattered to the first sub-step (\code{g_interleaved}) and both
gates are turned into chunk-local cumulative sums (\code{chunk_local_cumsum}, chunk size 64,
in base 2 via the constant \code{RCP_LN2}).
\item The WY representation is computed on the \emph{interleaved} sequence:
\code{chunk_scaled_dot_kkt_fwd} builds the strictly lower-triangular matrix $\mT$,
\code{solve_tril} inverts $\mI+\mT$, and \code{recompute_w_u_fwd} forms the analogues of
the two terms of \eqref{eq:c06-ut}.
\item \code{chunk_gated_delta_product_fwd_h} walks over the interleaved chunks of 64
sub-steps, updates the state and stores the pseudo-values \code{v_new}, but writes the state
to memory only when \code{i_t} is a multiple of \code{num_householder}, i.e.\ once every 64
\emph{real} tokens.
\item \code{chunk_fwd_kernel_o} works on chunks of 64 real tokens. It reads the stored state
for the inter-chunk term and adds, in a loop \texttt{for i\_dp in range(num\_householder)},
the intra-chunk contribution of every sub-step's key and pseudo-value. The causal mask
compares \emph{token} positions with \code{>=}, so a query sees all $n_h$ sub-steps of its own
token, which is the ``read after the last step'' rule.
\end{enumerate}
Why may stage 4 use pseudo-values that were computed relative to the state at the start of an
\emph{interleaved} chunk, while it only adds the state at the start of the \emph{real} chunk?
Because the state update telescopes: the state at the start of each interleaved chunk is the
previous one plus exactly the decayed $\vu\vk\T$ terms of that chunk, so summing all terms
since the start of the real chunk gives the same result. The backward pass simply calls
\code{chunk_gated_delta_rule_bwd} on the interleaved sequence; a comment in \code{chunk.py}
says ``TODO: optimize the backward pass like the forward pass''.
\end{implnote}

\begin{lstlisting}[caption={Excerpt from \texttt{repos/\allowbreak{}fla-org\_\_flash-linear-attention/\allowbreak{}fla/\allowbreak{}ops/\allowbreak{}gated\_delta\_product/\allowbreak{}naive.py}},label={lst:c06-dp-naive}]
    for i in range(T):
        if g is not None:
            h = h * g[:, i, :].exp()[..., None, None]
        # multiple state transition
        for j in range(num_householder):
            k_ij = k[:, i*num_householder+j, :, :]
            v_ij = v[:, i*num_householder+j, :, :]
            beta_ij = beta[:, i*num_householder+j, :]
            h = h + (v_ij - (h * k_ij[..., None]).sum(-2)).unsqueeze(-2) * k_ij[..., None] * beta_ij[..., None, None]
        # memory readout
        q_i = q[:, i, :, :]
        o_i = (h * q_i[..., None]).sum(-2)
        o[:, i] = o_i
\end{lstlisting}

With $h=\mS\T$, the update line reads $\mS\gets\mS+\beta(\vv-\mS\vk)\vk\T$, which is
\eqref{eq:c06-dp-step}.

\subsection{Reference implementation}

\begin{lstlisting}[caption={Reference implementation (ours): DeltaProduct recurrence and the interleaving map},label={lst:c06-dp-ours}]
import torch
import torch.nn.functional as F

def deltaproduct(q, k, v, beta, log_alpha, S=None):
    """One head. q: [T, dk]; k: [T, nh, dk]; v: [T, nh, dv];
    beta: [T, nh] in (0, 2); log_alpha: [T] (<= 0). State S: [dv, dk]."""
    T, nh, dk = k.shape
    k = F.normalize(k, dim=-1)            # unit keys: eigenvalues 1 and 1 - beta
    S = k.new_zeros(v.shape[-1], dk) if S is None else S
    out = []
    for t in range(T):
        S = log_alpha[t].exp() * S        # gate once per token
        for j in range(nh):               # n_h online gradient steps
            err = S @ k[t, j] - v[t, j]   # prediction error, [dv]
            S = S - beta[t, j] * torch.outer(err, k[t, j])
        out.append(S @ q[t])              # read after the last step
    return torch.stack(out), S

def interleave(q, k, v, beta, log_alpha):
    """Map DeltaProduct inputs to a gated delta rule over T * nh positions."""
    T, nh, dk = k.shape
    q_i = q.new_zeros(T, nh, dk); q_i[:, -1] = q              # read at last step
    g_i = log_alpha.new_zeros(T, nh); g_i[:, 0] = log_alpha  # decay at first step
    flat = lambda x: x.reshape(T * nh, *x.shape[2:])
    return flat(q_i), flat(k), flat(v), flat(beta), flat(g_i)

\subsection{Discussion}

\textbf{Strengths.} DeltaProduct turns the eigenvalue argument of
\cref{sec:c06-background} into a single, interpretable dial. It reuses the delta-rule
kernels unchanged (by interleaving), keeps the state size fixed, and its theory is clean:
products of reflections reach rotations, permutations and, with $n_h=d_k$, all orthogonal
matrices. The ``several gradient steps per token'' reading connects it to test-time
training (\cref{ch:ttt}), where taking more inner steps is a standard way to fit the
in-context objective better.

\textbf{Limitations.} Compute and projection parameters grow linearly in $n_h$, and the
interleaved sequence is $n_h$ times longer, which also lengthens the sequential part of the
chunkwise scan. The Householder factors of one token are generated in parallel from the same
input, so the model must learn $n_h$ coordinated keys; whether language data needs
rotations of high rank is an empirical question. Exact reflections require
$\beta_{t,j}$ to reach the boundary value 2 of a sigmoid, so in practice tracking is
approximate and relies on read-out tolerance and finite length.

\textbf{Relations.} With $n_h=1$ it is Gated DeltaNet (\cref{ch:delta}). Complex-valued
diagonal transitions such as Mamba-3's rotary updates (\cref{ch:structured}) also produce
rotations, but rotations that commute with one another: they can count modulo $m$ but cannot
represent non-abelian groups. DeltaProduct's rotations act in input-dependent planes and do
not commute. In hybrid models (\cref{ch:hybrid}) a few attention layers can supply exact
retrieval while expressive recurrent layers supply state tracking.

\section{Comba: scalar-plus-low-rank transitions with closed-loop control}\label{sec:c06-comba}

\subsection{Motivation}

Comba~\citep{hu2025comba} starts from a classification of recurrent memories. In the
authors' terms (their own summary is in
\repofile{repos/AwesomeSeq__Comba-triton/comba_chinese/paper.md}), \emph{linear RNNs}
such as GLA, HGRN2 and Mamba-2 update the state with data-dependent gates but never multiply
the state by the key; \emph{bilinear RNNs} such as Gated DeltaNet and RWKV-7 contain a
product of the state with the current key ($\mS_{t-1}\vk_t\vk_t\T$), so the dynamics are
linear in the state for fixed inputs and linear in the inputs for a fixed state, but not
jointly linear; \emph{nonlinear RNNs} such as TTT-MLP use deep memories updated by gradients
and lose cheap chunkwise parallelism. (Strictly speaking, an input-dependent gate
$\alpha_t\mS_{t-1}$ is also a state--input product; the distinction the authors draw is
whether the \emph{key} feeds back on the state.) The name ``bilinear'' comes from control
theory, where a bilinear system has dynamics
$\dot\vx=\mA\vx+\sum_iu_i\bm{N}_i\vx+\mB\vu$, linear in the state $\vx$ and in the control
$\vu$ separately.

From the control viewpoint, the memory $\mS$ is the \emph{plant}, the value $\vv_t$ is the
\emph{reference} the plant should reproduce for the key $\vk_t$, and the question is whether
the controller uses \emph{feedback}. Mamba-2 is an open-loop system: it writes $\vv_t$
without looking at what the memory currently returns. The delta rule closes the loop on
the input side: it writes the error $\vv_t-\mS_{t-1}\vk_t$. Comba closes the loop twice,
on the input with a \emph{state feedback} and on the output with an \emph{output feedback},
and it does so with a scalar-plus-low-rank (SPLR) transition that the authors argue is both
expressive enough and cheaper to parallelize than the alternatives.

\begin{taxonomy}
Following \citet{zhoubian2026memory}: \textbf{representation} implicit (recurrent matrix
state); \textbf{update dynamics} online; \textbf{persistence}: Comba is discussed in the
survey's \S III-C (``\ldots Comba uses scalar-plus-low-rank transitions and closed-loop
feedback'') but not listed in Table~I; by analogy with Gated DeltaNet it would be
long-term (our classification); \textbf{update rule} (Table~II): state-transition update
with feedback correction.
\end{taxonomy}

\subsection{Formulation}\label{sec:c06-comba-formulation}

\paragraph{The recurrence.}
The paper writes Comba as (this form is reproduced as an image in
\repofile{repos/AwesomeSeq__Comba-triton/comba_chinese/figure/eq1.png})
\begin{equation}\label{eq:c06-comba}
  \mS_t=\mS_{t-1}\big(\alpha_t\mI-\tilde\beta_t\vk_t\vk_t\T\big)+\beta_t\vv_t\vk_t\T,
  \qquad
  \vo_t=\mS_t(\vq_t-d\,\vk_t),
\end{equation}
with the first term labelled ``state correction'' and the second ``output correction''. In
the code the feedback vector is a separate $\bm{p}_t$, proportional to the key:
$\bm{p}_t=\sigmoid(\lambda)\vk_t$ with a learned scalar $\lambda$ per head (\code{decay}). With
$\bm{p}_t$ the general form, which we use from now on, is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:c06-comba-p}
  \vu_t=\beta_t(\vv_t-\mS_{t-1}\bm{p}_t),\qquad
  \mS_t=\alpha_t\mS_{t-1}+\vu_t\vk_t\T=\mS_{t-1}\big(\alpha_t\mI-\beta_t\bm{p}_t\vk_t\T\big)+\beta_t\vv_t\vk_t\T,
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:c06-comba-out}
  \vo_t=\mS_t\vq_t-d\,\mS_t\bm{p}_t=\mS_t(\vq_t-d\,\bm{p}_t).
\end{equation}
Read \eqref{eq:c06-comba-p} as a control loop. The \emph{state feedback}
$P(\mS)=\mS\bm{p}_t$ measures what the memory currently associates with the (feedback) key,
and the controller writes only the residual $\vv_t-P(\mS_{t-1})$, scaled by the gain
$\beta_t$. The \emph{output feedback} in \eqref{eq:c06-comba-out} subtracts $d$ times the
same measurement taken on the new state. The authors' Table~3 (reproduced in
\repofile{repos/AwesomeSeq__Comba-triton/comba_chinese/figure/control.png}) lines up the three
cases: Mamba-2 has no feedback and $\vo_t=\mS_t\vq_t$; the gated delta rule has
$\vv_t^{\text{new}}=\vv_t-\alpha_t\mS_{t-1}\vk_t$ and $\vo_t=\mS_t\vq_t$; Comba has
$\vv_t^{\text{new}}=\vv_t-P_t(\mS_{t-1})$ and $\vo_t=\mS_t\vq_t-dP_t(\mS_t)$. In the
authors' view, models with only the first correction (TTT, DeltaNet, RWKV-7) are not
strictly closed-loop.

\paragraph{Which transition does the code implement?}
There are two natural orderings of ``decay'' and ``feedback'', and they give different
models:
\begin{align}
  \text{SPLR (feedback, then decay):}\quad
  &\mS_t=\mS_{t-1}\big(\alpha_t\mI-\beta_t\bm{p}_t\vk_t\T\big)+\beta_t\vv_t\vk_t\T,\label{eq:c06-splr}\\
  \text{decay first:}\quad
  &\mS_t=\alpha_t\mS_{t-1}\big(\mI-\beta_t\bm{p}_t\vk_t\T\big)+\beta_t\vv_t\vk_t\T.\label{eq:c06-decayfirst}
\end{align}
The second is Gated DeltaNet's ordering. Comparing with \eqref{eq:c06-comba}, SPLR
corresponds to $\tilde\beta_t=\beta_t$ (with $\bm{p}_t=\vk_t$) and Gated DeltaNet to
$\tilde\beta_t=\alpha_t\beta_t$; our check confirmed both identities numerically, and also
that the two orderings agree when $\alpha_t=1$ and differ otherwise. The maintained \fla{}
kernels and the official ``\code{dplr}'' kernels implement SPLR \eqref{eq:c06-splr}; the
official repository also contains an ``\code{iplr}'' variant with the decay-first ordering
and $\beta_t\in(0,2)$ (\cref{sec:c06-comba-code}).

\paragraph{Eigenvalues.}
For a unit key and $\bm{p}_t=\vk_t$ (the case of the \fla{} kernels, see below), the SPLR
transition $\alpha_t\mI-\beta_t\vk_t\vk_t\T$ has eigenvalue $\alpha_t$ on every direction
orthogonal to $\vk_t$ and $\alpha_t-\beta_t$ along $\vk_t$ (the computation of
\eqref{eq:c06-hh-eig}). With $\alpha_t,\beta_t\in(0,1)$ the second eigenvalue lies in
$(-1,1)$, and it is \emph{negative whenever $\beta_t>\alpha_t$}, without doubling $\beta$.
Gated DeltaNet's corresponding eigenvalue is $\alpha_t(1-\beta_t)\in[0,1)$. Our check: for
$(\alpha,\beta)=(0.5,0.9)$ the SPLR eigenvalues are $\{0.5,-0.4\}$ while Gated DeltaNet gives
$0.05$. There is a catch (our analysis): a strongly negative eigenvalue requires
$\alpha_t$ small, and $\alpha_t$ also multiplies every other direction of the memory. SPLR
cannot produce a clean reflection (eigenvalue $-1$ along $\vk_t$ and $+1$ elsewhere) the
way DeltaNet with $\beta=2$ can; it trades sign flips against forgetting. The decay-first
variant with $\beta_t\in(0,2)$ has eigenvalue $\alpha_t(1-\beta_t)\in(-\alpha_t,\alpha_t)$
along the key and can approach a reflection when $\alpha_t\to1$. Like DeltaNet, both are
rank-one corrections with real spectra, so a single step cannot rotate.

\paragraph{What the output correction does.}
Right after a write with $\alpha_t=\beta_t=1$ and unit $\bm{p}_t=\vk_t$, the memory returns the
fresh value exactly: $\mS_t\vk_t=\mS_{t-1}(\vk_t-\vk_t)+\vv_t=\vv_t$ (checked numerically).
The read-out \eqref{eq:c06-comba-out} is then $\mS_t\vq_t-d\,\vv_t$: for $d>0$ it removes a
multiple of what the current token just stored, so the output emphasizes information
retrieved by the query \emph{beyond} the current key--value pair. Mamba-2's skip term
$D\vx_t$ adds a multiple of the current input; Comba's correction is its feedback analogue,
and the \fla{} warning text makes the comparison explicit (``\code{correction_factor} is set
to 1 by default similar to Mamba2''). The authors also give an objective view, listing
Comba's per-step loss as
$\beta_t\norm{\vv_t-\mS_t\vk_t}^2+\frac{\alpha_t}{2}\norm{\mS_t}^2+\inner{\vq_t}{d\vk_t}$
in their Table~1 (\repofile{repos/AwesomeSeq__Comba-triton/comba_chinese/figure/table1.png}).

\paragraph{Chunkwise form with a single inverse.}
We now derive the chunkwise algorithm for SPLR in our notation; the result has the same
structure as the paper's equations (9)--(12), which are reproduced in
\repofile{repos/AwesomeSeq__Comba-triton/comba_chinese/figure/eq2.png} and \code{eq3.png}.
Take a chunk of length $C$ with start state $\mS_{[i]}$ and positions $r=1,\dots,C$. Let
$\gamma_r=\prod_{s\le r}\alpha_s$ ($\gamma_0=1$). From \eqref{eq:c06-comba-p}, the state is
the decayed start state plus decayed rank-one writes,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:c06-comba-state}
  \mS_r=\gamma_r\mS_{[i]}+\sum_{s\le r}\frac{\gamma_r}{\gamma_s}\vu_s\vk_s\T .
\end{equation}
Insert this (at $r-1$) into $\vu_r=\beta_r\vv_r-\beta_r\mS_{r-1}\bm{p}_r$:
\begin{equation}
  \vu_r+\beta_r\sum_{s<r}\frac{\gamma_{r-1}}{\gamma_s}(\bm{p}_r\T\vk_s)\,\vu_s
  =\beta_r\vv_r-\beta_r\gamma_{r-1}\mS_{[i]}\bm{p}_r .
\end{equation}
Stack $\vu_r\T$ as the rows of $\mU\in\R^{C\times d_v}$ and define the strictly
lower-triangular $\mT_{rs}=\beta_r(\gamma_{r-1}/\gamma_s)\bm{p}_r\T\vk_s$ ($s<r$). Then
$(\mI+\mT)\mU=\diag(\vbeta)\mV-\diag(\vbeta\had\bm{\gamma}_{-})\mP\mS_{[i]}\T$, where
$\bm{\gamma}_-=(\gamma_0,\dots,\gamma_{C-1})$. With the \emph{single} inverse
$\mM=(\mI+\mT)^{-1}$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:c06-comba-wu}
  \mW=\mM\diag(\vbeta\had\bm{\gamma}_-)\mP,\qquad \mU^{0}=\mM\diag(\vbeta)\mV,\qquad
  \mU=\mU^{0}-\mW\mS_{[i]}\T .
\end{equation}
The outputs and the next chunk state follow from \eqref{eq:c06-comba-state}, with
$\tilde\mQ$ the rows $(\vq_r-d\bm{p}_r)\T$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:c06-comba-out-chunk}
  \mO_{[i]}=\diag(\bm{\gamma})\tilde\mQ\mS_{[i]}\T+\big((\tilde\mQ\mK\T)\had\bm{\Gamma}\big)_{\le}\mU,
  \qquad
  \mS_{[i+1]}=\gamma_C\mS_{[i]}+\mU\T\diag(\gamma_C/\bm{\gamma})\mK,
\end{equation}
with $\bm{\Gamma}_{rs}=\gamma_r/\gamma_s$. Both $\mW$ (which multiplies the incoming state) and
$\mU^0$ (which carries the values) use the same $\mM$. This is the point the authors
emphasize: their summary attributes Comba's forward-pass speed to needing one inverse where
their Gated DeltaNet formulation needs two. In the decay-first ordering the coefficient of
$\vu_s$ in $\vu_r$ is $\gamma_r/\gamma_s$ rather than $\gamma_{r-1}/\gamma_s$; the official
reference code for that variant (\code{chunk_comba_iplr_ref}) indeed builds two triangular
matrices, one with and one without the decay. (The current \fla{} Gated DeltaNet forward pass also
uses a single triangular matrix, returned by \code{chunk_gated_delta_rule_fwd_intra} in
\code{fla/ops/gated_delta_rule/chunk.py}, so the size of the advantage depends on the baseline
implementation.) Our NumPy implementation of
\eqref{eq:c06-comba-wu}--\eqref{eq:c06-comba-out-chunk} matched the SPLR recurrence to
$2\times10^{-16}$ for chunk sizes 4, 8 and 16, also for a general $\bm{p}_t$ not parallel to
$\vk_t$.

\subsection{Algorithms and complexity}

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Comba (SPLR, one head): chunkwise forward pass}
\label{alg:c06-comba}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require $\mQ,\mK,\mP\in\R^{T\times d_k}$, $\mV\in\R^{T\times d_v}$, gains $\vbeta$, log-decays $\log\valpha$, factor $d$, chunk size $C$
\State $\tilde\mQ\gets\mQ-d\,\mP$ \Comment{output feedback}
\State $\mS\gets\bm 0$
\For{each chunk $[i]$}
  \State $\bm{\gamma}\gets$ cumulative products of $\alpha$ in the chunk; $\bm{\gamma}_-\gets(1,\gamma_1,\dots,\gamma_{C-1})$
  \State $\mT\gets\mathrm{strictlower}\big(\diag(\vbeta)\,((\mP\mK\T)\had[\gamma_{r-1}/\gamma_s]_{rs})\big)$
  \State $\mM\gets(\mI+\mT)^{-1}$ \Comment{forward substitution, $\bigO(C^3)$ or blocked}
  \State $\mW\gets\mM\diag(\vbeta\had\bm{\gamma}_-)\mP$;\quad $\mU\gets\mM\diag(\vbeta)\mV-\mW\mS\T$
  \State $\mO_{[i]}\gets\diag(\bm{\gamma})\tilde\mQ\mS\T+((\tilde\mQ\mK\T)\had\bm{\Gamma})_{\le}\mU$
  \State $\mS\gets\gamma_C\mS+\mU\T\diag(\gamma_C/\bm{\gamma})\mK$
\EndFor
\State \Return $\mO$, $\mS$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\Cref{alg:c06-comba} costs per head $\bigO(TC(d_k+d_v)+Td_kd_v)$ for the matrix products
and $\bigO(TC^2)$ for the triangular solves, the same order as Gated DeltaNet. Decoding uses
\eqref{eq:c06-comba-p}--\eqref{eq:c06-comba-out} directly: two matrix--vector products, one
rank-one update and one read, $\bigO(d_kd_v)$ time per token; the state is $d_kd_v$ per
head. Comba adds essentially no parameters: one scalar $\lambda$ (feedback scale) and one
scalar $d$ (output correction) per head.

\subsection{Implementation walkthrough}\label{sec:c06-comba-code}

\begin{implnote}[title={In the code: the official repository}]
\repofile{repos/AwesomeSeq__Comba-triton/fla/layers/comba.py} defines \code{Comba} with
q/k/v projections, short convolutions, a per-head gate \code{a_proj} (Mamba-2
parameterization with \code{A_log} and \code{dt_bias}), a per-head \code{b_proj} for
$\beta_t$, a per-head parameter \code{decay} (initialized to ones) and the correction factor
\code{D} (initialized to \code{1e-2}). The feedback vector is \texttt{p = k * decay.sigmoid()}
and, if \code{qk_sim} is set, the query is corrected as \texttt{q - D * p}. The argument
\code{A_type} selects the kernel family: \code{'iplr'} uses $\beta_t=2\sigmoid(\cdot)$ and the
decay-first ordering \eqref{eq:c06-decayfirst} (kernels in
\code{fla/ops/comba/comba_iplr/}); \code{'dplr'} uses $\beta_t=\sigmoid(\cdot)$ and the SPLR
ordering \eqref{eq:c06-splr} (\code{fla/ops/comba/comba_dplr/}). The two orderings are visible
in the fused recurrent kernels: in \code{comba_iplr/fused_recurrent.py} the line
\texttt{b\_h *= exp(b\_g)} precedes \texttt{b\_v -= tl.sum(b\_h * b\_p[:, None], 0)}, in
\code{comba_dplr/fused_recurrent.py} it follows it. Training configurations
(\code{training/configs/comba_iplr_340M.json}, \code{comba_dplr_340M.json}) use 24 layers,
\code{hidden_size} 1024 and 4 heads of dimension 256, and differ only in \code{A_type}.
\end{implnote}

\begin{lstlisting}[caption={Excerpt from \texttt{repos/\allowbreak{}fla-org\_\_flash-linear-attention/\allowbreak{}fla/\allowbreak{}layers/\allowbreak{}comba.py}},label={lst:c06-comba-fla}]
        q, k = map(lambda x: rearrange(x, '... (h d) -> ... h d', d=self.head_k_dim), (q, k))

        if self.use_inner_decay:

        else:
            p = k

\begin{implnote}[title={In the code: the maintained \fla{} version}]
The \fla{} layer (\cref{lst:c06-comba-fla}) exposes the two ideas as switches:
\code{use_inner_decay} (feedback vector $\bm{p}_t=\sigmoid(\lambda)\vk_t$) and
\code{use_output_correction} with \code{correction_factor} (default 1, initial value of

Both kernels normalize $\vq$, $\vk$ \emph{and} $\bm{p}$ to unit length inside the kernel
(\code{l2norm_fwd(p)} in \code{ops/comba/chunk.py}; \texttt{b\_p / tl.sqrt(...)} in
\code{ops/comba/fused_recurrent.py}). Since $\bm{p}_t$ is a positive multiple of $\vk_t$, after
normalization $\hat{\bm{p}}_t=\hat\vk_t$: as we read the code, the transition is exactly
$\alpha_t\mI-\beta_t\hat\vk_t\hat\vk_t\T$, and the learned scale $\sigmoid(\lambda)$ survives
only in the output correction, which is applied to the \emph{unnormalized} query and key
before the kernel normalizes $\vq_t-D\sigmoid(\lambda)\vk_t$. The paper's exact
parameterization may differ. The Triton chunk path \code{chunk_comba_fwd} mirrors \cref{alg:c06-comba}:
\code{chunk_comba_cumsum_scalar_fwd} (\code{ops/comba/utils.py}) computes two chunk-local
cumulative sums of the log-gate, exclusive (\code{g0}) and inclusive (\code{g}) of the current
step, i.e.\ our $\log\bm{\gamma}_-$ and $\log\bm{\gamma}$;
\code{chunk_scaled_dot_comba_pkt_fwd} (\code{ops/comba/wy_fast.py}) builds $\mT$ from $\bm{p}$,
$\vk$, $\beta$ and both sums; the generic \code{solve_tril} inverts $\mI+\mT$ once;
\code{recompute_w_u_fwd} forms $\mW$ and $\mU^0$ (it is called with \code{k=p} and
\code{g_cumsum=g0}); and the generic gated-delta-rule kernels
\code{chunk_gated_delta_rule_fwd_h} and \code{chunk_fwd_o} compute the states and outputs.
\end{implnote}

In \cref{lst:c06-comba-kernel}, \code{b_h} is the state tile $\mS\T$ of shape
\texttt{[BK, BV]}; the feedback \texttt{b\_v -= ...} uses the state \emph{before} the decay
\texttt{b\_h *= exp(b\_g)}, which is the SPLR ordering \eqref{eq:c06-splr}.

\begin{pitfall}[title={Pitfall: two reference recurrences}]
In \repofile{repos/fla-org__flash-linear-attention/fla/ops/comba/naive.py}, the function
\code{naive_recurrent_comba} applies the decay \emph{before} the feedback
(\texttt{h = h.clone() * g[:, :, i].exp()[...]} precedes \texttt{b\_v = b\_v - ...}), i.e.\ the
decay-first ordering \eqref{eq:c06-decayfirst}, whereas \code{naive_chunk_comba} and the
Triton kernels implement SPLR. The \fla{} tests (\code{tests/ops/test_comba.py}) compare the
kernels with \code{naive_chunk_comba} only. Our NumPy versions of the two orderings differ by
up to $0.18$ in the outputs on random inputs and agree exactly when $\alpha_t\equiv1$. When
porting Comba, decide which ordering you want and test against the matching reference.
\end{pitfall}

\subsection{Reference implementation}

\begin{lstlisting}[caption={Reference implementation (ours): Comba recurrence (SPLR) and chunkwise form},label={lst:c06-comba-ours}]
import torch

def comba_recurrent(q, k, v, p, beta, log_alpha, d, S=None):
    """One head, unit q, k, p: [T, dk]; v: [T, dv]; beta, log_alpha: [T]."""
    S = k.new_zeros(v.shape[1], k.shape[1]) if S is None else S
    out = []
    for t in range(k.shape[0]):
        u = beta[t] * (v[t] - S @ p[t])               # state feedback on S_{t-1}
        S = log_alpha[t].exp() * S + torch.outer(u, k[t])
        out.append(S @ (q[t] - d * p[t]))             # output feedback
    return torch.stack(out), S

def comba_chunk(q, k, v, p, beta, log_alpha, d, C=64):
    T, dk = k.shape
    S = k.new_zeros(v.shape[1], dk)
    q, out = q - d * p, []
    for c in range(0, T, C):
        Q, K, V, P = q[c:c+C], k[c:c+C], v[c:c+C], p[c:c+C]
        b, g = beta[c:c+C], log_alpha[c:c+C]
        G = g.cumsum(0); G0 = G - g                    # log gamma_r, log gamma_{r-1}
        T_ = torch.tril(b[:, None] * (G0[:, None] - G[None, :]).exp() * (P @ K.T), -1)
        M = torch.linalg.inv(torch.eye(len(G)) + T_)   # the single inverse
        W = M @ (b[:, None] * G0.exp()[:, None] * P)
        U = M @ (b[:, None] * V) - W @ S.T             # pseudo-values
        A = torch.tril((G[:, None] - G[None, :]).exp() * (Q @ K.T))
        out.append(G.exp()[:, None] * (Q @ S.T) + A @ U)
        S = G[-1].exp() * S + (U * (G[-1] - G).exp()[:, None]).T @ K
    return torch.cat(out), S
\end{lstlisting}

The NumPy version of \cref{lst:c06-comba-ours} is the one whose agreement we reported above.
Note that the exponent in \code{T_} is masked to the strictly lower triangle \emph{after}
exponentiation; for long chunks, production kernels instead keep the cumulative log-decays
bounded (sub-chunking, base-2 exponentials) to avoid overflow in
$\exp(\log\gamma_{r-1}-\log\gamma_s)$ above the diagonal.

\subsection{Discussion}

\textbf{Strengths.} Comba changes very little relative to Gated DeltaNet, two scalars per
head and the order of two lines, yet the SPLR ordering gives the transition a negative
eigenvalue range without doubling $\beta$, and the single-inverse chunk form is simple. The
control-theory reading gives names to design choices (feedback on the input, feedback on
the output) that are otherwise easy to overlook, and it suggests further variants, e.g.\
feedback vectors $\bm{p}_t$ that are not parallel to the key; the chunk algorithm
above handles any $\bm{p}_t$.

\textbf{Limitations.} The transition is still identity-like plus rank one, with a real
spectrum; for state tracking it is in the DeltaNet class, not the DeltaProduct class. In the
maintained kernels the feedback key is normalized to the key itself, so the ``closed loop''
on the state side reduces to the delta rule with an SPLR decay. The recommended $d$ depends on
scale, a sign that the output correction interacts with normalization and depth in ways
not yet well understood.

\textbf{Relations.} Comba's SPLR transition sits between Gated DeltaNet (decay-first,
eigenvalues in $[0,1)$) and KDA/RWKV-7 (per-channel diagonal); the output feedback is
orthogonal to the transition and could be added to any of the models in this chapter or in
\cref{ch:delta}. Gated DeltaNet-2 (\cref{ch:delta}) likewise decouples erasing from
writing, by splitting the single strength $\beta_t$ into a key-side erase gate and a value-side
write gate.

\section{RWKV-7 ``Goose'': a generalized delta rule with vector gates}\label{sec:c06-rwkv7}

\subsection{Motivation: from RWKV-5/6 to RWKV-7}

RWKV is a line of attention-free language models trained in parallel like Transformers and
run as RNNs at inference~\citep{c06-peng2023rwkv}. RWKV-5 (``Eagle'') and RWKV-6
(``Finch'')~\citep{peng2024eagle} introduced matrix-valued states. In our notation, RWKV-6
updates each head as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:c06-rwkv6}
  \mS_t=\mS_{t-1}\diag(\vw_t)+\vv_t\vk_t\T,\qquad
  \vo_t=\big(\mS_{t-1}+\vv_t(\vu\had\vk_t)\T\big)\vr_t ,
\end{equation}
with a per-channel decay $\vw_t\in(0,1)^{d_k}$ (fixed in RWKV-5, data-dependent in RWKV-6), a
learned ``bonus'' $\vu$ for the current token and a query called the \emph{receptance}
$\vr_t$. (This is the recurrence of the \fla{} reference
\code{naive_recurrent_rwkv6}.) The transition is diagonal with positive entries, so by
\cref{prop:c06-parity} it cannot track parity, and the memory can only fade, never be
selectively overwritten. RWKV-7~\citep{peng2025rwkv7} keeps the per-channel decay and adds
the delta rule, generalized in three ways:
\begin{enumerate}
\item the erase strength becomes a \emph{vector} $\va_t\in(0,1)^{d_k}$, the
\emph{in-context learning rate}, one rate per key channel;
\item the key used to \emph{remove} old content ($\hat{\bm\kappa}_t$) is decoupled from the
key used to \emph{write} new content ($\tilde\vk_t$);
\item the decay $\vw_t$ is vector-valued and is applied together with the low-rank
correction, giving a diagonal-plus-low-rank transition.
\end{enumerate}
The survey summarizes it as a generalized delta rule ``with vector-valued state gates and
in-context learning rates while decoupling removal and addition keys, enabling channel-wise
state replacement''~\citep{zhoubian2026memory}.

\begin{taxonomy}
Following \citet{zhoubian2026memory} (Table~I: ``Vector-Gated Generalized Delta
Recurrence''): \textbf{representation} implicit; \textbf{update dynamics} online;
\textbf{persistence} long-term; \textbf{update rule} (Table~II): state-transition update,
a generalized delta rule with vector-valued decay and learning rate.
\end{taxonomy}

\subsection{Formulation}\label{sec:c06-rwkv-formulation}

We follow the reference code
(\repofile{repos/BlinkDL__RWKV-LM/RWKV-v7/train_temp/src/model.py} and the RNN-mode demo
\repofile{repos/BlinkDL__RWKV-LM/RWKV-v7/rwkv_v7_demo_rnn.py}). RWKV calls its
sequence mixer \emph{time mixing} and its MLP \emph{channel mixing}. Let
$\vx_t\in\R^{C}$ be the (layer-normed) input of the time-mixing block, $C=d$ the model
width, split into $H$ heads of size $N=C/H$ (the reference code asserts $N=64$), so per head
$d_k=d_v=N$.

\paragraph{Token shift.} Every branch first mixes the current token with the previous one,
using a learned per-channel interpolation weight $\bm\mu_c\in\R^C$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:c06-tokenshift}
  \vx^{c}_t=\vx_t+\bm\mu_c\had(\vx_{t-1}-\vx_t),\qquad c\in\{r,w,k,v,a,g\}.
\end{equation}
This is a two-tap causal convolution, the RWKV counterpart of the short convolutions in
Gated DeltaNet and DeltaProduct: it gives every key, value and gate direct access to a
bigram of tokens.

\paragraph{Projections and gates.} With $\mW_r,\mW_k,\mW_v\in\R^{C\times C}$ and small
low-rank (LoRA) maps $\mW_{\cdot1}\in\R^{C\times D}$, $\mW_{\cdot2}\in\R^{D\times C}$:
\begin{align}
  \vr_t&=\mW_r\vx^r_t,\quad \vk_t=\mW_k\vx^k_t,\quad \vv_t=\mW_v\vx^v_t,\\
  \vw_t&=\exp\!\big(-e^{-1/2}\,\sigmoid(\vw_0+\tanh(\vx^w_t\mW_{w1})\mW_{w2})\big)
        \in\big(e^{-e^{-1/2}},1\big)^C\approx(0.545,1)^C, \label{eq:c06-rwkv-decay}\\
  \va_t&=\sigmoid(\va_0+\vx^a_t\mW_{a1}\mW_{a2})\in(0,1)^C,\\
  \vg_t&=\sigmoid(\vx^g_t\mW_{g1})\mW_{g2}.
\end{align}
(We write row vectors times matrices where the code does.) The decay
\eqref{eq:c06-rwkv-decay} is a soft clamp: $-\log w=e^{-1/2}\sigmoid(\cdot)$ lies in
$(0,e^{-1/2})$, so no channel can lose more than a factor $e^{-0.6065}\approx0.545$ per token.
The RNN demo also gives the equivalent ``naive'' form $w=\exp(-\exp(\tilde w))$ with
$\tilde w=-\mathrm{softplus}(-(\vw_0+\cdot))-0.5$; since $-\mathrm{softplus}(-z)=\log\sigmoid(z)$,
both are the same. In layers after the first, the value is pulled toward the first layer's
value (a ``value residual''):
$\vv_t\gets\vv_t+(\vv^{(1)}_t-\vv_t)\had\sigmoid(\vv_0+\vx^v_t\mW_{v1}\mW_{v2})$.

\paragraph{Removal and replacement keys.} Per head, with learned vectors $\bm\xi$
(\code{k_k}) and $\bm\zeta$ (\code{k_a}):
\begin{equation}\label{eq:c06-rwkv-keys}
  \hat{\bm\kappa}_t=\frac{\vk_t\had\bm\xi}{\norm{\vk_t\had\bm\xi}},\qquad
  \tilde\vk_t=\vk_t\had\big(\bm1+(\va_t-\bm1)\had\bm\zeta\big).
\end{equation}
The \emph{removal key} $\hat{\bm\kappa}_t$ is unit-norm, as the delta rule needs. The
\emph{replacement key} $\tilde\vk_t$ interpolates per channel between $\vk_t$ (when
$\zeta_i=0$) and $\vk_t\had\va_t$ (when $\zeta_i=1$, the delta rule's ``write with the same
rate you erase''); $\bm\zeta$ is initialized at $1.02$. The paper calls this a relaxed value
replacement rule.

\paragraph{State update and read-out.} Per head,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:c06-rwkv7}
  \boxed{\;\mS_t=\mS_{t-1}\big(\diag(\vw_t)-\hat{\bm\kappa}_t(\va_t\had\hat{\bm\kappa}_t)\T\big)+\vv_t\tilde\vk_t\T,
  \qquad \vy_t=\mS_t\vr_t\;}
\end{equation}
and the block output is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:c06-rwkv-out}
  \vo_t=\mW_o\Big[\Big(\mathrm{GroupNorm}(\vy_t)+\big(\textstyle\sum_{j}r_{t,j}\tilde k_{t,j}\rho_j\big)\vv_t\Big)\had\vg_t\Big],
\end{equation}
where the group norm normalizes each head separately and the scalar
$\sum_jr_{t,j}\tilde k_{t,j}\rho_j$ (learned $\bm\rho$, \code{r_k}, one vector per head) is a
``bonus'' that adds the current value directly, the successor of RWKV-6's $\vu$ term.

\paragraph{Reading \eqref{eq:c06-rwkv7} as a delta rule.}
Expand the transition:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:c06-rwkv-expanded}
  \mS_t=\mS_{t-1}\diag(\vw_t)-\big(\mS_{t-1}\hat{\bm\kappa}_t\big)(\va_t\had\hat{\bm\kappa}_t)\T+\vv_t\tilde\vk_t\T .
\end{equation}
The vector $\mS_{t-1}\hat{\bm\kappa}_t$ is what the memory currently returns for the
removal key; it is subtracted along the direction $\hat{\bm\kappa}_t$, channel by channel at
rate $\va_t$, and the new value is written along the replacement key. With $\vw_t=\bm1$,
$\va_t=\beta_t\bm1$, $\bm\zeta=\bm1$ and a unit $\vk_t=\hat{\bm\kappa}_t$ this is exactly
DeltaNet, $\mS_t=\mS_{t-1}(\mI-\beta_t\vk_t\vk_t\T)+\beta_t\vv_t\vk_t\T$ (our check: agreement
to $2\times10^{-15}$). The \fla{} derivation note
\repofile{repos/fla-org__flash-linear-attention/fla/ops/rwkv7/RWKV7(Goose).md} presents the
same reading as gradient descent with weight decay: on $\loss=\tfrac12\norm{\vv-\mS\vk}^2$,
$\mS_t=\mS_{t-1}\mD_t-\eta_t(\mS_{t-1}\vk_t\vk_t\T-\vv_t\vk_t\T)$, whose term
$-\eta_t\vk_t\vk_t\T$ RWKV-7 generalizes to a general rank-one product. Note also the
ordering: the removal is measured on the \emph{undecayed} state, as in Comba's SPLR
\eqref{eq:c06-splr}, whereas KDA decays first.

\paragraph{The rest of the block.} A layer computes
$\vx\gets\vx+\mathrm{TimeMix}(\LayerNorm_1(\vx))$ and then
$\vx\gets\vx+\mathrm{ChannelMix}(\LayerNorm_2(\vx))$ (the first layer also normalizes the
embeddings with an extra $\LayerNorm_0$). Channel mixing is a token-shifted MLP with a squared
ReLU and hidden width $4C$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:c06-cmix}
  \vx^k_t=\vx_t+\bm\mu_k\had(\vx_{t-1}-\vx_t),\qquad
  \mathrm{ChannelMix}(\vx)_t=\mW^{\text{ffn}}_v\,\ReLU(\mW^{\text{ffn}}_k\vx^k_t)^2 .
\end{equation}

\subsection{Eigenvalues of the RWKV-7 transition}\label{sec:c06-rwkv-eig}

The transition $\mA_t=\diag(\vw_t)-\hat{\bm\kappa}_t(\va_t\had\hat{\bm\kappa}_t)\T$ is not
symmetric when $\va_t$ is not constant, so a priori its eigenvalues could be complex or
large. The following analysis is ours; the paper's own expressivity results are summarized
after it.

\begin{proposition}[Spectrum of the RWKV-7 transition]\label{prop:c06-rwkv-eig}
Let $\vw\in(0,1]^{d}$, $\va\in(0,1)^{d}$ and $\norm{\hat{\bm\kappa}}=1$. Then
$\mA=\diag(\vw)-\hat{\bm\kappa}(\va\had\hat{\bm\kappa})\T$ is similar to the symmetric matrix
$\diag(\vw)-\bm{c}\bm{c}\T$ with $\bm{c}=\sqrt{\va}\had\hat{\bm\kappa}$. Hence all eigenvalues of $\mA$
are real and lie in $[\min_iw_i-\norm{\bm{c}}^2,\ \max_iw_i]\subset(\min_iw_i-1,\ 1]$. With the
clamp \eqref{eq:c06-rwkv-decay} this interval is contained in $(-0.455,\,1)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $\mP=\diag(\sqrt{\va})$, which is invertible because every $a_i>0$. Diagonal matrices
commute, hence $\mP\diag(\vw)\mP^{-1}=\diag(\vw)$. For the rank-one part,
\[
  \mP\hat{\bm\kappa}(\va\had\hat{\bm\kappa})\T\mP^{-1}
  =(\sqrt{\va}\had\hat{\bm\kappa})\,(\va\had\hat{\bm\kappa}\had\va^{-1/2})\T=\bm{c}\bm{c}\T .
\]
So $\mP\mA\mP^{-1}=\diag(\vw)-\bm{c}\bm{c}\T$, which is symmetric, and similar matrices have the same
eigenvalues. For the bounds, $\bm{c}\bm{c}\T$ is positive semidefinite with largest eigenvalue
$\norm{\bm{c}}^2$, so by Weyl's inequality every eigenvalue of $\diag(\vw)-\bm{c}\bm{c}\T$ lies between
$\min_iw_i-\norm{\bm{c}}^2$ and $\max_iw_i$. Finally
$\norm{\bm{c}}^2=\sum_ia_i\hat\kappa_i^2<\sum_i\hat\kappa_i^2=1$, and the clamp gives
$\min_iw_i>e^{-e^{-1/2}}\approx0.5452$.
\end{proof}

Three consequences. First, a single RWKV-7 step has a \emph{real} spectrum, like DeltaNet and
Comba, so it cannot rotate. Second, negative eigenvalues are possible, down to about
$-0.45$: put $\hat{\bm\kappa}_t$ on a channel $i$ with the strongest allowed decay and
$a_{t,i}\to1$; then that direction gets $w_i-a_i\approx0.545-1$ while channels with $w\approx1$
are kept. Per-channel decay is what makes this possible: unlike Comba's SPLR, the decay of
the flipped direction is decoupled from the decay of the rest of the memory. Third, the
clamp keeps the spectrum away from $-1$, so an exact reflection is not available in one step.
Our numerical check sampled 20{,}000 random transitions: the largest imaginary part of any
eigenvalue was exactly zero, all eigenvalues obeyed the bounds of
\cref{prop:c06-rwkv-eig}, the observed range was $[-0.389,1.000]$, and the extreme
construction above gives $-0.4548$.

\paragraph{What the paper proves.} The RWKV-7 abstract states that RWKV-7 ``can perform state
tracking and recognize all regular languages, while retaining parallelizability of
training'', which exceeds what Transformers can do under the standard conjecture
TC$^0\ne$ NC$^1$ (\cref{sec:c06-complexity}). These are statements about stacks of complete
RWKV-7 layers and about products of transitions over several tokens; products of
non-commuting real-spectrum matrices can have complex eigenvalues, so the single-step bound
above does not limit them. See the paper for the constructions.

\paragraph{Chunkwise form for DPLR transitions.}
Training can use a chunkwise algorithm that generalizes the UT transform to DPLR transitions.
We derive it for $\mS_t=\mS_{t-1}(\mD_t+\bm\phi_t\bm\psi_t\T)+\vv_t\vk_t\T$, with RWKV-7 as
the case $\mD_t=\diag(\vw_t)$, $\bm\phi_t=-\hat{\bm\kappa}_t$,
$\bm\psi_t=\va_t\had\hat{\bm\kappa}_t$, $\vk_t\to\tilde\vk_t$, $\vq_t=\vr_t$. Define the
``removal read-out'' $\vz_t=\mS_{t-1}\bm\phi_t$, so that
$\mS_t=\mS_{t-1}\mD_t+\vz_t\bm\psi_t\T+\vv_t\vk_t\T$. Inside a chunk with start state
$\mS_{[i]}$ let $\mLambda_r=\mD_1\cdots\mD_r$ (diagonal, cumulative within the chunk,
$\mLambda_0=\mI$). Unrolling,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:c06-dplr-state}
  \mS_r=\mS_{[i]}\mLambda_r+\sum_{s\le r}\big(\vz_s\bm\psi_s\T+\vv_s\vk_s\T\big)\mLambda_s^{-1}\mLambda_r .
\end{equation}
Multiply \eqref{eq:c06-dplr-state} (at $r-1$) by $\bm\phi_r$ and introduce the decayed vectors
$\hat{\bm\phi}_r=\mLambda_{r-1}\bm\phi_r$, $\bar{\bm\psi}_s=\mLambda_s^{-1}\bm\psi_s$,
$\bar\vk_s=\mLambda_s^{-1}\vk_s$, $\hat\vq_r=\mLambda_r\vq_r$:
\begin{equation}
  \vz_r=\mS_{[i]}\hat{\bm\phi}_r+\sum_{s<r}\big[(\bar{\bm\psi}_s\T\hat{\bm\phi}_r)\,\vz_s+(\bar\vk_s\T\hat{\bm\phi}_r)\,\vv_s\big].
\end{equation}
With rows stacked, the strictly lower-triangular matrices
$(\mA_{\phi\psi})_{rs}=\hat{\bm\phi}_r\T\bar{\bm\psi}_s$ and
$(\mA_{\phi k})_{rs}=\hat{\bm\phi}_r\T\bar\vk_s$ ($s<r$), and one triangular inverse,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:c06-dplr-z}
  \mZ=(\mI-\mA_{\phi\psi})^{-1}\big(\hat{\bm\Phi}\mS_{[i]}\T+\mA_{\phi k}\mV\big)
     =\mW\mS_{[i]}\T+\mU .
\end{equation}
Reading out $\vy_r=\mS_r\vq_r$ from \eqref{eq:c06-dplr-state}, with the lower-triangular
(including diagonal) matrices $(\mA_{q\psi})_{rs}=\hat\vq_r\T\bar{\bm\psi}_s$ and
$(\mA_{qk})_{rs}=\hat\vq_r\T\bar\vk_s$ ($s\le r$):
\begin{equation}\label{eq:c06-dplr-out}
  \mY_{[i]}=\hat\mQ\mS_{[i]}\T+\mA_{q\psi}\mZ+\mA_{qk}\mV,\qquad
  \mS_{[i+1]}=\big(\mS_{[i]}+\mZ\T\bar{\bm\Psi}+\mV\T\bar\mK\big)\mLambda_C .
\end{equation}
These are, up to notation, the quantities \code{A_ab}, \code{A_ak}, \code{A_qb}, \code{A_qk},
\code{w}, \code{u} computed by \fla{}'s \code{chunk_dplr_fwd}. Our NumPy implementation of
\eqref{eq:c06-dplr-z}--\eqref{eq:c06-dplr-out} matched the RWKV-7 recurrence to
$3\times10^{-15}$ for chunk sizes 4, 8 and 16. The factors $\mLambda_s^{-1}$ grow
exponentially inside a chunk, which is why production kernels bound the per-step log-decay
and the chunk length (next subsection).

\subsection{Algorithms and complexity}

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{RWKV-7 time mixing (one head): decoding step}
\label{alg:c06-rwkv7}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require state $\mS\in\R^{N\times N}$; previous input $\vx_{t-1}$; current input $\vx_t$
\State $\vx^c_t\gets\vx_t+\bm\mu_c\had(\vx_{t-1}-\vx_t)$ for $c\in\{r,w,k,v,a,g\}$ \Comment{token shift}
\State compute $\vr_t,\vk_t,\vv_t,\vw_t,\va_t,\vg_t$; value residual for layers after the first
\State $\hat{\bm\kappa}_t\gets\mathrm{normalize}(\vk_t\had\bm\xi)$;\quad $\tilde\vk_t\gets\vk_t\had(\bm1+(\va_t-\bm1)\had\bm\zeta)$
\State $\vz\gets\mS(-\hat{\bm\kappa}_t)$ \Comment{removal read-out on the old state, $\bigO(N^2)$}
\State $\mS\gets\mS\diag(\vw_t)+\vz(\hat{\bm\kappa}_t\had\va_t)\T+\vv_t\tilde\vk_t\T$ \Comment{$\bigO(N^2)$}
\State $\vy_t\gets\mS\vr_t$
\State $\vo_t\gets\mW_o[(\mathrm{GroupNorm}(\vy_t)+(\sum_jr_{t,j}\tilde k_{t,j}\rho_j)\vv_t)\had\vg_t]$
\State \Return $\vo_t$, $\mS$, $\vx_t$ \Comment{$\vx_t$ is the token-shift state}
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

Per head, the state is $N^2$ numbers plus one $C$-vector of token-shift state per block for
time mixing and one for channel mixing; decoding (\cref{alg:c06-rwkv7}) costs $\bigO(N^2)$
time per token. For training there are two strategies.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Sequential scan (official CUDA kernel).} One thread block per (batch, head) pair
and one thread per state row; each thread keeps its row of $\mS$ ($N=64$ floats) in registers
and loops over time. Work is $\bigO(TN^2)$ per head, parallel over $B\cdot H\cdot N$
threads, with sequential depth $T$. Because the per-token work is tiny and the state fits in
registers, this simple scan is fast in practice.
\item \textbf{Chunkwise DPLR (\fla{}).} \eqref{eq:c06-dplr-z}--\eqref{eq:c06-dplr-out} cost
$\bigO(TC N+TN^2)$ for the products plus $\bigO(TC^2)$ for the inverses, with depth $T/C$,
and map onto tensor cores.
\end{itemize}

\begin{implnote}[title={In the code: the CUDA recurrence}]
In \cref{lst:c06-rwkv-cuda}, thread \code{i} of block (\code{hh}, \code{bb}) owns row $i$ of
the state, \code{state[j]} $=S_{ij}$. The decay line computes
$w=\exp(c\,\sigmoid(\cdot))$ with $c=$ \code{W_SCALE} $=-e^{-1/2}=-0.6065306597$,

(hence ``clampw''). \code{sa} is the removal read-out $z_i=(\mS_{t-1}(-\hat{\bm\kappa}_t))_i$,

backward pass. After the loop shown, every \code{_CHUNK_LEN_} (16) steps the thread writes its
row into a checkpoint tensor \code{s} of shape \texttt{[B, H, T/16, N, N]}; the backward kernel
restarts from these checkpoints. The older kernel \code{wkv7_cuda.cu} in the same folder uses
the unclamped $w=\exp(-\exp(\cdot))$, and the inference kernels live in
\code{RWKV-v7/cuda/}.

\subsection{Reference implementation}

\begin{lstlisting}[caption={Reference implementation (ours): RWKV-7 time mixing, recurrent form},label={lst:c06-rwkv-ours}]
import math
import torch
import torch.nn.functional as F

def rwkv7_time_mix(x, P, H, v_first=None):
    """x: [T, C] after LayerNorm; P: dict of parameters (names as in the paper's
    code); H heads of size N = C // H. Returns [T, C] and v_first."""
    T, C = x.shape
    N = C // H
    xx = torch.cat([x.new_zeros(1, C), x[:-1]]) - x            # x_{t-1} - x_t
    xr, xw, xk, xv, xa, xg = (x + xx * P['mu_' + c] for c in 'rwkvag')
    r, k, v = xr @ P['Wr'], xk @ P['Wk'], xv @ P['Wv']
    d = P['w0'] + torch.tanh(xw @ P['w1']) @ P['w2']
    w = torch.exp(-math.exp(-0.5) * torch.sigmoid(d))          # decay in (0.545, 1)
    a = torch.sigmoid(P['a0'] + (xa @ P['a1']) @ P['a2'])       # in-context learning rate
    g = torch.sigmoid(xg @ P['g1']) @ P['g2']                   # output gate
    if v_first is None:
        v_first = v                                            # first layer
    else:
        v = v + (v_first - v) * torch.sigmoid(P['v0'] + (xv @ P['v1']) @ P['v2'])
    kap = F.normalize((k * P['k_k']).view(T, H, N), dim=-1)     # removal key
    k = k * (1 + (a - 1) * P['k_a'])                            # replacement key
    r, w, k, v, a = (z.view(T, H, N) for z in (r, w, k, v, a))
    S = x.new_zeros(H, N, N)                                    # [head, value, key]
    ys = []
    for t in range(T):
        z = torch.einsum('hvk,hk->hv', S, -kap[t])              # removal read-out
        S = (S * w[t][:, None, :]
             + torch.einsum('hv,hk->hvk', z, kap[t] * a[t])     # remove along kappa
             + torch.einsum('hv,hk->hvk', v[t], k[t]))          # write v along k~
        ys.append(torch.einsum('hvk,hk->hv', S, r[t]))          # read after write
    y = F.group_norm(torch.stack(ys).reshape(T, C), H, P['ln_w'], P['ln_b'], eps=64e-5)
    bonus = ((r * k * P['r_k']).sum(-1, keepdim=True) * v).reshape(T, C)
    return ((y + bonus) * g) @ P['Wo'], v_first
\end{lstlisting}

\Cref{lst:c06-rwkv-ours} follows the reference line by line, with one simplification: the
input $\vx$ is one sequence without a carried token-shift state. In NumPy we checked the
recurrence part against the matrix form \eqref{eq:c06-rwkv7} (agreement $2\times10^{-15}$),
against the DPLR chunkwise algorithm (\cref{sec:c06-rwkv-eig}), against DeltaNet in the special
case, and the eigenvalue bounds of \cref{prop:c06-rwkv-eig}.

\subsection{Discussion}

\textbf{Strengths.} RWKV-7 is the most fine-grained transition in this chapter:
per-channel decay, per-channel learning rate, and separate keys for erasing and writing,
all at the cost of a rank-one update per token. Its negative eigenvalues (down to about
$-0.45$) need no doubling of a gate, and its per-channel decay lets one direction flip sign
while the rest of the memory is preserved. The official CUDA scan shows that for head size
64 a plain sequential kernel can be competitive, and the released models make it one of
the most used recurrent architectures.

\textbf{Limitations.} The design depends on many carefully tuned initializations and
per-parameter learning-rate and weight-decay settings; the authors warn that a stand-alone
layer is hard to get right and that the \fla{} version is not aligned with theirs. Each step
is still rank one with a real spectrum and cannot rotate; per-token expressivity grows only
by composing tokens or layers.

\textbf{Relations.} Setting $\vw_t=\bm1$, $\va_t=\beta_t\bm1$ and tying the keys recovers
DeltaNet; tying the keys but keeping vector decay gives a KDA-like transition with a different
ordering (\cref{ch:delta}); DeltaProduct generalizes the other way, from rank one to rank
$n_h$. RWKV-7's ``generalized delta rule'' is exactly the DPLR family that \fla{} implements
once (\code{chunk_dplr_delta_rule}) and reuses.

\paragraph{Three ways to enrich a transition.} The systems move along different axes from
Gated DeltaNet. DeltaProduct raises the \emph{rank} of the correction (one to $n_h$), which is
the only route in this chapter to complex eigenvalues within a single token, and pays for it
linearly in compute. Comba changes the \emph{ordering} and \emph{scalar structure} (decay
after feedback, plus an output correction), which costs nothing and opens a negative
eigenvalue range that is tied to forgetting. RWKV-7 refines the \emph{per-channel structure}
(vector decay, vector learning rate, decoupled keys), which allows partial sign flips on
some channels while preserving others, at the price of more projections and a delicate
initialization.

\paragraph{Training cost.} All three keep a chunkwise algorithm with one triangular inverse
per chunk: DeltaProduct by interleaving into a gated delta rule (\cref{prop:c06-interleave}),
Comba by the single-inverse SPLR form \eqref{eq:c06-comba-wu}, RWKV-7 by the DPLR form
\eqref{eq:c06-dplr-z}. DPLR is the most general and the most delicate numerically because of
the inverse cumulative decays $\mLambda_s^{-1}$; RWKV-7's decay clamp doubles as a guarantee
that the kernel can use 64-token chunks.

\paragraph{Recall versus tracking.} The capacity arguments of \cref{ch:delta} (at most $d_k$
orthogonal keys can be stored without interference) are unchanged by richer transitions: a
reflection or rotation does not create room for more associations. What changes is what the
memory can \emph{compute} about the order of events. This is why expressive recurrent layers
combine naturally with a few attention layers in hybrids (\cref{ch:hybrid}): attention
provides exact retrieval over the context, the recurrent layers provide cheap, order-sensitive
state.

\paragraph{From theory to practice.} The theorems are about exact solutions at all lengths.
Trained models implement them only approximately: exact reflections need $\beta=2$ at the
boundary of a sigmoid, finite precision blurs long products, and a language model rarely
needs to compose a thousand permutations. The evidence in the repositories is therefore
qualitative but consistent: with too few Householder factors or with non-negative eigenvalues,
single-layer models fail exactly where \cref{prop:c06-perm} says they must, and the
DeltaProduct README reports better length extrapolation as $n_h$ grows.

\paragraph{A design checklist.} When building a recurrent memory layer: (i) let the
eigenvalue along the key go negative (doubling $\beta$, SPLR ordering, or per-channel decay);
(ii) decide whether single-token rotations are needed, and if so use a product of $n_h\ge2$
Householders; (iii) consider per-channel decay to decouple flipping one direction from
forgetting the others; (iv) treat output corrections and decoupled write keys as independent
add-ons; (v) verify the recurrence against a chunkwise kernel and a reference with the
\emph{same} ordering of decay and feedback.

\begin{summarybox}
\begin{itemize}
\item State tracking (parity, modular counting, permutation composition) is a memory
capability distinct from associative recall: the state must be transformed, not looked up.
A linear recurrence tracks a group exactly when its transitions can realize a representation
of the group.
\item Diagonal transitions with non-negative entries cannot track parity: every channel
converges monotonically. A single eigenvalue $-1$ fixes parity; non-commuting transitions are
needed for groups like $S_5$, whose word problem is NC$^1$-complete, beyond the TC$^0$ class
that contains log-precision Transformers and diagonal SSMs.
\item A generalized Householder $\mI-\beta\vk\vk\T$ has eigenvalues $1$ and $1-\beta$;
$\beta\in(0,2)$ gives $[-1,1]$ and corresponds to stable gradient steps that overshoot.
Products of Householders give rotations and permutations; a permutation with $c$ cycles on
$n$ objects needs exactly $n-c$ reflections.
\item DeltaProduct takes $n_h$ delta steps per token, so each transition is a product of
$n_h$ Householders; it trains as a gated delta rule over an interleaved sequence of length
$Tn_h$ and tracks $S_{n_h+1}$ in one layer, at $n_h$ times the compute and the same state size.
\item Comba applies feedback before decay (SPLR, $\alpha_t\mI-\beta_t\bm{p}_t\vk_t\T$) and corrects
the output with $-d\,\mS_t\bm{p}_t$; its chunk form needs one inverse. In the \fla{} kernels the
feedback key is normalized to the key, and two reference recurrences use different orderings.
\item RWKV-7 uses $\diag(\vw_t)-\hat{\bm\kappa}_t(\va_t\had\hat{\bm\kappa}_t)\T$ with a clamped
vector decay, a vector learning rate and separate removal and replacement keys; each step has
real eigenvalues in about $(-0.45,1)$. The official code is a sequential CUDA scan; \fla{}
offers a DPLR chunkwise version that its authors do not consider aligned.
\end{itemize}
\end{summarybox}

\section*{Exercises}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Exercises}

\begin{exercise}[Householder spectrum and overshooting]\label{ex:c06-householder}
Let $\norm{\vk}=1$ and $\mH=\mI-\beta\vk\vk\T$.
(a) Prove that $\mH$ has eigenvalue $1-\beta$ with eigenvector $\vk$ and eigenvalue 1 on
$\vk^\perp$, and that $\norm{\mH}_2\le1$ if and only if $\beta\in[0,2]$.
(b) Derive \eqref{eq:c06-residual} and conclude that, for repeated writes of the same pair
$(\vk,\vv)$, the retrieval error $\mS_t\vk-\vv$ converges to zero for every
$\beta\in(0,2)$, alternating in sign when $\beta>1$.
(c) What happens to the error at $\beta=2$, and what happens to the part of the state
orthogonal to $\vk$?
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[Two reflections make a rotation]\label{ex:c06-rotation}
Let $\vk_1,\vk_2\in\R^d$ be unit vectors with $\vk_1\T\vk_2=\cos\theta$.
(a) In $d=2$ write $\vk_i=(\cos\theta_i,\sin\theta_i)$ and show that
$\mH(\vk_1,2)\mH(\vk_2,2)$ is the rotation by $2(\theta_1-\theta_2)$.
(b) For general $d$, show that the product fixes every vector orthogonal to both keys and
acts on $\mathrm{span}\{\vk_1,\vk_2\}$ as a rotation by $\pm2\theta$; deduce its eigenvalues.
(c) Give keys and step sizes for a DeltaProduct$_2[-1,1]$ layer with $d_k=2$ that counts
modulo 3: the state should return to its initial value after every third ``1'' token and be
unchanged by ``0'' tokens.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[Positive diagonal recurrences and parity]\label{ex:c06-parity}
(a) Complete the proof of \cref{prop:c06-parity} for a read-out $y_t=f(\vh_t)$ given by a fixed
ReLU network followed by a threshold. (Hint: a ReLU network is piecewise linear with finitely
many pieces.)
(b) Extend the argument to a stack of two such layers, assuming finite precision: show that
the first layer's state becomes exactly constant after finitely many steps on $1^n$, and
conclude.
(c) Show that one channel with $a(1)=-1$ solves parity, and explain in one sentence why this
does not contradict TC$^0$ lower bounds.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[Tracking $S_3$]\label{ex:c06-s3}
Let $\mP$ be the permutation matrix of the 3-cycle $(1\,2\,3)$.
(a) Show that $\mP\neq\mI-\beta\vk\vk\T$ for every unit $\vk$ and every real $\beta$.
(b) Write $\mP$ as a product of two reflections $\mH(\vk_1,2)\mH(\vk_2,2)$ with explicit keys.
(c) Show that a product of two generalized Householders with $\beta_1,\beta_2\in[0,1]$ has
only real, non-negative eigenvalues (hint: each factor is positive semidefinite, and $\mA\mB$
is similar to $\mA^{1/2}\mB\mA^{1/2}$ when $\mA$ is invertible). Relate (a)--(c) to the four
$S_3$ configurations in the DeltaProduct README.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[RWKV-7 eigenvalues]\label{ex:c06-rwkv}
(a) Prove \cref{prop:c06-rwkv-eig} in detail, including the case where some $a_i$ equals one.
(b) For $d_k=2$, $\hat{\bm\kappa}=(1,0)$, $\vw=(w_1,w_2)$ and $\va=(a_1,a_2)$, compute the
eigenvalues explicitly. When is one of them negative? What is the most negative value allowed
by the clamp \eqref{eq:c06-rwkv-decay}?
(c) Show that if $\va_t=\beta_t\bm1$ the transition is symmetric, and compare its spectrum with
that of KDA's $\diag(\valpha_t)(\mI-\beta_t\vk_t\vk_t\T)$ for the same inputs.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}[Coding: chunkwise forms]\label{ex:c06-code}
(a) Implement \cref{alg:c06-comba} in NumPy and check it against
\cref{lst:c06-comba-ours} (recurrent part) for random inputs, chunk sizes 4 to 64 and both
$\bm{p}_t=\vk_t$ and a general $\bm{p}_t$.
(b) Derive the chunkwise form of the decay-first ordering \eqref{eq:c06-decayfirst}: which
$\gamma$ index changes in $\mT$ and in $\mW$? Implement it and confirm that it differs from
SPLR unless $\alpha_t\equiv1$.
(c) Implement DeltaProduct prefill by interleaving (\cref{lst:c06-dp-ours}) on top of your
chunkwise gated delta rule. Count floating-point operations as a function of $n_h$, $C$,
$d_k$, $d_v$ and $T$ and compare with $n_h$ separate DeltaNet layers.
\end{exercise}
